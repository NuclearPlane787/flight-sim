## Decided in the Cowork session

These are design rules found while checking the defaults against the code. Change one only with a reason, and log it.

- **Korea unchanged, exactly:**
  - every cell of Korea's 250 m grid inside the boundary its OpenStreetMap extract was cut with (Geofabrik's `south-korea.poly`) keeps its height and water to the bit;
  - so do the 30 m patches (`incheon-gimpo`, `jeju`) and the 10 m coast patches (`RKSS`, `RKSI`, `RKPC`);
  - `heightAt()` over South Korea returns the same doubles as v0.5.8. The exceptions are Gimhae's new patch and its runways' flattening, and within a cell of the boundary a re-baked neighbour may differ, which only matters at the DMZ;
  - the three golden flights to 1e-9 are the proof, and the golden test files may change only in how they load the terrain (they read `data/manifest.json`'s national grid directly today);
  - at the end of Part 1, before anything changes Korea's look on purpose, Korea's stills at Gimpo, Jeju and Incheon by day look the same as v0.5.8's (a pixel diff, shown).

  Korea's cover (and so its cities and trees) stays until Parts 6 and 7, which change Korea's look on purpose. Its heights and water never change.
- **Bit-exact sampling:** keep Korea's lattice for the whole region: 250 m cells, cell-centred, with the origin at Korea's south-west corner (−400 km, −400 km), extended both ways. Compute the cell index as today, `fx = (x − X0) / 250 − 0.5` with that origin, then the tile and the cell within it by integer arithmetic, and keep the same bilinear expression. Clamp only at the region's outer edge. (A per-tile origin would round differently.) Tiles of 512 cells (128 km) with a border of their neighbours' cells (2 is enough for the Catmull-Rom water taps and the normals) mean no sample ever straddles two tiles.
- **The sim never sees a missing tile.** The sim, the gear and the prediction read the same data a player's flight does, or saves, rewinds and jumps stop being exact.
  - The loader keeps the 250 m tiles round the aircraft resident (and the camera's, and the departure's and arrival's) and loads ahead along the track.
  - If a frame's steps would reach a tile that isn't in yet, those steps wait for it (silently; "Loading terrain…" after 0.5 s).
  - A jump waits for its target's tiles behind its fade; a new flight or a loaded scenario waits for its own.
  - In the margin (1 km data only), the sim and the worker both read the 1 km level. That's consistent, because there is no 250 m data there.
- **The prediction worker** loads the tiles a chunk will reach before it runs the chunk (3,000 steps at 1/30 s is about 25 km at cruise), and the destination's at the first request. It never samples a coarse stand-in where the live sim would read 250 m, and its 2 s CPU budget doesn't count the waiting.
- **Magnetic variation:** `magVar()` returns today's plane exactly inside `south-korea.poly`; the golden flights fly magnetic headings (`ap.setHdg(170)`), so this isn't optional. Elsewhere it's WMM2025 (public domain, NOAA NCEI's `WMM.COF`) on a baked grid at epoch 2026.0, blended from the plane across a band of about 50 km just outside the poly. The new airports' `magVarDeg` and their runways' magnetic headings come from the grid; Gimhae's, inside the poly, from the plane. For reference, the AIPs give Haneda and Narita 8° W and Pudong 5°47′ W (2017), and the plane extrapolates to 7.1–7.3° W at all three.
- **The sky's north:** `sky.js` places the sun, the Moon and the stars by true azimuth (`azElToScene()`, `j2000ToEnuMatrix`) on a map whose north is the grid's. Turn them by the grid convergence at the camera (`convergence(lon)` in `frame.js`: −0.41° at Gimpo, −3.31° at Pudong, +7.13° at Haneda, +7.49° at Narita, ±9 to 12° at the margin's edges). Korea's sun moves by 0.4°, which won't show in the stills: show the diff anyway.
- **The wind, true everywhere:** `weather.js` turns the set wind into map coordinates once, at the arrival (`refX`, `refY`, about line 247), and `clouds.js` does the same for the drift (about line 741). Over 7.5° of grid turn between Gimpo and Haneda, that leaves the departure's tower wind 6–7° off.
  - Keep the wind's true direction as set at the arrival (its magnetic direction there, plus the variation there), and turn it into grid coordinates at each point by the local convergence. This stays a function of position, seeded, with no new state.
  - With the weather `null` nothing changes (the golden flights).
  - With weather, the wind at the arrival is exactly v0.5.8's. Weather tests far from the arrival may move a fraction of a degree: update those expectations and log each one.
- **Precision far from the origin:**
  - The shaders get map coordinates as `uOrigin + scene position` in float32 (`sceneToMap()` in `glsl.js`). At Haneda and Narita (x ≈ 1,110–1,160 km) that's 0.125 m steps, 8× coarser than at Gimpo, and the fine procedural detail suffers: `vnoise(m * 1.3)`, the field plots, the street grid, the paint.
  - Look at the ground close up at both airports, at Kansai and at the margin's edge. Where it's blocky or swims, split the origin: a part snapped to a large power of two, fixed while you're in an area so patterns don't pop, plus a small remainder, or the noise lattice computed from a CPU-side integer offset.
  - Part 7's mosaic must be built this way from the start.
  - The sim is in doubles and is fine.
- **The uniform arrays:**
  - `MAX_PATCHES` (4: the 30 m patches, about 10 of them now, and separately the 10 m coasts) and `MAX_RUNWAYS` (12; the eleven airports have 29 runways, Pudong alone 5) become the nearest ones to the camera, refreshed as it moves.
  - The CPU's `heightAt()` keeps all of them. Flattening is local, so the GPU and the CPU agree wherever the camera can see.
  - Raise a cap only if nearest-N can't work, and say why.
- **The clipmap's horizon cull:** `PEAK` in `terrain.js` is 2,000 m (Hallasan). Fuji is 3,776 m, so raise `PEAK` (or make it the highest ground in reach) or Fuji's rings are culled while it should still be in sight from cruise. The landmarks' heights (Shanghai Tower 632 m, Skytree 634 m) are buildings, not terrain: cull them by their own height.
- **`MAP_EXTENT`** (`frame.js`) becomes the region's. The predicted path ends at it (`predict-core.js`), and the map draws it (`map.js`).
- **The airports are built near the camera:** eleven airports' meshes, lights, signs and traffic shouldn't all be resident. Build each one within about 200 km and release it beyond about 400 km. Pudong and Hongqiao, and Haneda and Narita, will be built together. Measure the time to build each and log it. The draw-call budget is about 300.
- **CPU and memory at runtime** (`PLAN.md` → CPU at runtime):
  - decompress and prepare tiles (normals, borders) off the main thread, and upload at most about a tile's worth to the GPU per frame;
  - nothing runs while paused or hidden;
  - the resident set stays about Korea's today (10 M cells at 250 m) plus the 1 km level, Part 7's fine cover window and the real centres' buildings near the camera: a bounded cache, tiles released behind you.
- **The data in git:** tiles, patches, the fine cover and the centres' buildings in `data/`, each file under 100 MB. The total is about 80–150 MB more than v0.5.8's 13 MB (the fine cover is the biggest part); more than about 200 MB needs a reason in `STATUS.md`. Bake each tile once: every re-bake of a committed tile grows the repository's history. Korea's three national files go once nothing reads them; the run can delete files on the Mac (the Cowork shell can't).
- **Landmarks are ours:** massing models made here from public facts (height, floors, footprint, the taper and the crown's shape), with no logos, signage or names on them, and no models or textures copied from anywhere. The same goes for the real centres: OpenStreetMap's footprints and heights (ODbL, credited), our own facades.
- **Credits:** in the README, beside the Copernicus DEM and OSM credits already there:
  - ESA WorldCover (CC BY 4.0: "© ESA WorldCover project 2021 / Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data (2021) processed by ESA WorldCover consortium");
  - OpenStreetMap's land polygons and buildings (ODbL, with the existing OSM credit);
  - WMM and Natural Earth, which are public domain, named anyway.

## The rules every part keeps

- **Korea's heights and water don't change** (above): the three golden flights to 1e-9, and all 1024 unit tests with their assertions unchanged. Only the terrain's loaders in tests may change, and so may weather expectations far from the arrival, each logged.
- **Saves stay exact:** `sim.snapshot()` round-trips through JSON. A scenario saved at Haneda, at Pudong or at Gimhae restores and flies 120 s against the original within 1e-6 m (as `tests/scenario-state.test.mjs` does for Korea), and a v0.5.8 save loads and flies as it did.
- **Nothing random in the sim:** anything new that the sim or the prediction reads (the country, the transition, the variation, the wind's turn) is a function of position, never of `Math.random()`, the frame, or what happens to be loaded. The look's procedural detail (fields, buildings, trees) is seeded by map position, the same every run.
- **Night needs every ground shader** (`HANDOFF.md`'s pitfalls), and night-only objects hide by day. Clear weather draws as before. New objects on the ground (landmarks, the centres, rural buildings) follow the pavement and runway rules there: nothing on a runway or in its strip, nothing lifted over one.
- **Storage:** every key starts `gamedump:flight-sim:`.
- **Keys:** nothing new in the warehouse's block (7–0, U–P, J–;, M–/ and Shift with them). Anything new goes on the Controls page, and by Shane's rule a key pressed more than once a flight in the air needs no Shift. The BARO's unit and the metric altitude are clicks on the FCU and the EFIS panel, not keys. No Ctrl bindings (Cmd/Ctrl+L stays the only one).
- **No network at runtime:** the game reads only its own folder. Downloads happen in the bake.
- **The repository is public:**
  - no third-party documents (the AIP pages and charts you read go in `reference/docs/`, git-ignored);
  - no third-party images, models or sounds;
  - no absolute paths with the Mac's user name;
  - no real airline's branding, and no company's logo or signage on a building.
