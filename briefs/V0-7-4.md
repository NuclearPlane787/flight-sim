## The parts, in priority order

### 1. Flying: items 8, 7, 9 and 6

#### Item 8: the off-centreline score (`src/score.js`)

- **Today:** `COST.centreline: (cross) => 0.25 * soft(cross, 4)`, scored by `scoreFor(cost, 0.16)`. So 1 m off scores 10, 2 m 8, 5 m 4 and 10 m 1.
- **Make:**
  - 10 within 1.5 m of the centreline;
  - then a straight line to 0 where the outer main wheel's outer edge reaches the runway's edge, at (runway width / 2) − (main gear track / 2 + the outer wheel's offset and half its width);
  - 0 beyond that.
  - Use the gear of the type or design being flown (the warehouse moves it), and the runway's own width. That is about 18 m for the A321 on a 45 m runway, 16 m for the 777, and more on 60 m runways.
  - Shane's 5.7 m in an A321 on a 45 m runway then scores 7 of 10 instead of about 3.
- **Unchanged:** the part's weight in the total, the would-be crash rule and the card's shares. The logbook keeps the scores it recorded.
- **Tests:**
  - 1.5 m gives 10, and the edge 0;
  - 5.7 m (A321, 45 m runway) gives 7;
  - a 60 m runway;
  - a design with its gear moved out.

#### Item 7: the autothrust's full range

- **From the code:**
  - 96.5 % is the A321neo's N1 at full thrust (`N1_TOGA` in `src/sim/a321neo.js`). That is real, not a cap. The 787's is 97 % and the 777's 100 %.
  - The Boeings' A/T wake-up is capped at MCT: the SPD case in `src/sim/autopilot-boeing.js` has `const limit = X.boWake ? Math.max(A.RATING.MCT, ref) : ref`.
  - It also takes the lever from where it is, so a wake-up with the levers at full pulls them back to MCT. That is "the automatic low speed activation actually slows the plane".
- **Make:**
  - **The Boeings' wake-up goes to full thrust** (`A.RATING.TOGA`). While awake, it never sets less thrust than the levers had when it woke. Once the speed is back within 2 kt of its target (the exit, as now), the SPD mode's own limit (the reference) applies again.
  - **The A321:** alpha floor already gives TOGA LK. Its A/THR in SPEED with the levers in CL keeps the CL limit, as the real one does.
  - **"Full" reads as full on every type:**
    - the engine panel shows the thrust limit's mode and its N1 above the dials, as the real displays do (the A321's E/WD: "TOGA 96.5"; the Boeings' EICAS: the reference mode and N1);
    - the lever ring stands at the limit;
    - so a needle at 96.5 is visibly at the limit.
- **Tests:**
  - a wake-up from idle reaches TOGA;
  - a wake-up with the levers at TOGA doesn't reduce thrust;
  - once the speed has recovered, the reference returns;
  - the three types' golden flights are unchanged (log any that change, and why).

### 4. Weather by place (v0.6.5)

**Read first:** `HANDOFF.md`'s section "After it, or when Shane says: v0.6.5, weather by place". It has what the code has today and the questions, each with a first thought; this part takes those first thoughts as the decisions.

**From the code** (that section):
- One settings object covers the whole flight (`src/sim/weather.js`: `calmWeather()`, `normalizeWeather()`, `compileWeather()`, `windAt()`).
- The wind's true direction is set at the arrival and turned to each place's north (`windTurnAt()`), so it is one wind everywhere.
- Everything is a function of position and time, with a seed and no state: the live sim (1/120 s), the predicted path (1/30 s) and a jump's restore all read the same air. **That must stay true.**
- The presets and the METAR line are in `src/sim/weather-presets.js`: `presetWeather()`, `weatherContext()`, a severe storm's microbursts placed near airports, and `metarOf()` in each country's form.
- The weather is set in three places: New scenario's preset (`ui/setup.js`), the Weather tab (`ui/weather-window.js`, `applyWeather()` in `main.js`), and `sim.setWeather()`.

**Make** (Shane: "Yes, set both ends"):
- **Two weathers, the departure's and the destination's.**
  - New scenario picks both; the destination's defaults to the departure's.
  - The Weather tab gets a Departure / Destination switch, and each end can be changed in flight.
  - A save or a scenario from before v0.7.4 loads as the same weather at both ends.
- **Between the ends, a blend by position only,** stateless, so the prediction and the jumps stay exact:
  - each end's weather holds near its airport (for example over the first and last thirds of the route);
  - the blend runs smoothly across the middle;
  - beyond the ends, the nearer end's weather holds.
  - The wind (by height), the gusts and the turbulence, the visibility, the cloud layers, the rain and snow, the temperature and the QNH all blend, so the altimeter setting matters en route.
  - A storm's microbursts and lightning stay near the end that has the storm.
