## The parts, in priority order

### 1. The towns: Europe's real centres baked again

From "Found while building v0.7.0" and "… v0.7.1", and v0.7.6's pictures-only review.

**The findings:**
- **The centres look alike.** From 3,000 ft, each new centre is a small real core inside the same courtyard blocks under pink roofs, with a visible ring where the real footprints stop.
  - Toulouse's suburbs should be low houses.
  - Dijon's town, beyond the 31 m cover, reads as white courtyard rings (v0.7.0's French mix).
  - Zaragoza's far bank is one courtyard block, repeated.
- **Courtyards are roofed over:** the centres' format keeps only a building's outer ring.
- **Town blocks stand in narrow rivers,** such as the Main below the Commerzbank Tower and the Thames by Tower Bridge. The 250 m water field can't hold a river 100 to 200 m wide. The Ebro is wide and soft-edged close up.

**Make:**
- **Inner rings:** the centres' format keeps a building's inner rings (its courtyards), and the bake writes them. Every real centre in Europe is baked again with them, and the game draws the courtyard open.
- **No ring at the edge:** the generated town blends into the real centre over a band, instead of stopping at a line. Its mix varies by place: the country's own mixes, with the block size, height and roof colour varied by distance from the centre and by a seeded noise.
- **Low suburbs:** past the centre, low houses, not courtyard blocks. Toulouse, Dijon and Zaragoza are the test cases.
- **Rivers clear of blocks:** a finer water mask for the towns, from OSM's river and canal areas (riverbank, water polygons), so that no generated block stands in a mapped river. Draw the rivers' edges from it where the 250 m water field is too coarse.
- **The size:** keep each scene under about a million triangles at the views in the stills (`?cull=0` to compare), and log `data/`'s growth.

**Check:**
- Frankfurt, London, Paris, Toulouse, Dijon, Zaragoza and three more centres from 3,000 ft, before and after;
- the Main and the Thames close up;
- 60 fps at Heathrow's and Frankfurt's stands and finals, on mains and alone.

### 2. The countryside

From "Found while building v0.7.0" to "… v0.7.6".

**The findings:**
- **The hedged and dry zones follow latitude and longitude, not borders** (`euGroundGlsl()`, wobbled by a slow noise). The hedges stop near the Loire, and the dry country starts at about 43.3° N, whatever the country.
- **The polders' strips** are crossed by blurry bands of the land cover's grassland, and their direction changes at a straight seam every 8 km.
- **A faint brightness step** may show where the hedged fields give way to their far colour.
- **Lyon's flat near-white strips:** WorldCover's bare ground (class 6) is drawn as bare-or-snow (v0.7.3; v0.7.4's part 9 left it).
- **Zaragoza's airfield grass** is a hard-edged green rectangle on dry ground (v0.7.6).

**Make:**
- **Each country's fields by the country raster** (`data/europe/country.u8.gz`, already loaded for the rules) in the ground shader, with the zone's edge softened over a few kilometres along the border.
  - Hedged pasture: the British Isles, Normandy and Brittany.
  - Open fields: France's north and middle, Germany, the Low Countries' arable land.
  - Dry fields and olives: Spain, Portugal, Italy's south, southern France.
  - Polder strips: the Netherlands' polders.

  The zones within a country stay as now where a country has more than one.
- **The polders:** the strips' direction comes from the nearest drainage line, or a slow field, not from 8 km cells. The grassland class under a polder draws as the polder's own strips, not as a blurred band.
- **Bare ground** is tinted by height, or by the urban mask, so that it isn't snow below the snow line or in a town.
- **An airfield's grass** takes the ground's dry or green tone from its place, with a soft edge.
- **East Asia** keeps its own ground; check that it hasn't changed.

**Check:**
- the Loire's border to the hedges;
- the Pyrenees between France and Spain;
- Ireland, Kent and Normandy;
- the Alentejo;
- Flevoland's polders from 3,000 ft;
- Lyon;
- Zaragoza's airfield;
- East Asia's sixteen views at max diff 0.

### 3. The ground's data

From "Found while building v0.7.0" and "… v0.7.1".

**The findings:**
- **Inland water sits 2 m under its banks with no floor,** so the IJsselmeer and the Markermeer lie at about −6 to −8 m instead of about −0.2 m. Polder lakes are baked 2 m under banks that are themselves at −5 to −6 m.
- **Schiphol's 30 m patch has radar noise at big buildings:** 710 cells (0.64 km²) down to −19.5 m where Amsterdam's stadium stands, 11 km east of the field, and smaller clusters at −12 to −15 m.
- **Cristo Rei stands on 31 m of game ground** at its coordinates. The real cliff is 133 m above the river, and the 250 m heights smooth it away.

**Make:**
- **Lakes at their own level:** a water body's surface is set from its mapped level (OSM's `ele` tags) or its shore's median height, not 2 m under the bank, with a floor so that it never reads as sea. The IJsselmeer and the Markermeer come out at about −0.2 m.
- **Schiphol's patch** gets a floor at the polders' real depth (about −7 m), so that the noise is gone.
- **Cristo Rei's cliff:** a 30 m patch round it from GLO-30, so that the statue stands on its cliff above the Tagus.
- **The bake:** re-bake only the tiles and patches these touch, sealed and proved as the batches were:
  - seams bit-identical;
  - every core that isn't meant to change proved by hash;
  - installed by `tools/install-region.py`;
  - sizes logged.

**Check:**
- the IJsselmeer from 3,000 ft and at its shore;
- Schiphol's final;
- Cristo Rei and the 25 de Abril bridge from the river;
- Europe's views unchanged away from the re-baked tiles, with `--total`.
