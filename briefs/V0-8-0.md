## The Cowork session's choices (told to him; his to change)

- **A failure plan is the world's, a failure's state the aircraft's.** A rewind and a jump keep the weather set now (`restore(snap, { keepWeather: true })`); they keep the armed failures the same way, while the core restores which have fired and how far each has gone. So a failure set for 10:00 fires again at 10:00 after a rewind to 09:30, and the predicted path, a rewind and a jump agree.
- **A random failure stays hidden:** the Failures tab says one is armed and its window, not what or when; the predicted path doesn't show it; a jump past its moment ends at the last sample before it, and the failure fires there.
- **A gear-up landing within limits is survivable whether a failure caused it or the gear was forgotten** (row 9's "land without gear"). Past the limits it is a crash, as now; Indestructible keeps its own behaviour.
- **An engine fire's odds are seeded per failure:** out with the first bottle most times, with the second some, with neither rarely (`[EST]`). A fire that isn't put out stays a warning to the landing: structural damage waits for v0.12.0's crash physics.

### 2. The failure model

**From the code:**
- **Two failures exist today:**
  - an engine stops when the fuel runs out (`engines.js`, `fuelOk`: N1 to the windmill's 4 + 20 M, no thrust, no fuel flow);
  - an engine tears off when its pylon breaks (`sim.js`, a bit of `X.eOff`; an `engineOff` event; a toast in `main.js`; "ENG n OFF" in amber on the engine panel and the EICAS).
- **The core `X` is the aircraft's state,** copied whole by `snapshot()` and back by `restore()` (older saves' missing fields given defaults there); `snap.weather` and `snap.options` sit beside it.
- **A rewind and a jump** call `restore(snap, { keepWeather: true })` (`main.js`: `rewindNow()`, `jumpTo()`). The predictor gets `sim.snapshot()` (`predict.js`: `request(snap)`), and a jump restores a sample's snapshot. A scenario saves `sim.snapshot()` (`captureState()`).
- **The rules** (HANDOFF's pitfalls):
  - saves stay deterministic (`tests/scenario-state.test.mjs` flies a restored sim 120 s against the original, within 1e-6 m);
  - nothing random in the sim: anything that varies is a function of place and time with a seed;
  - calm weather is v0.3.2's arithmetic to 1e-9 (the golden flights).

**Make:**
- **A failure** is what, which and when:
  - **what:** an engine's flameout, seizure or fire; the gear's normal extension failed, a leg that won't lock, a leg that collapses on the next landing, the gravity extension failed too;
  - **which:** the engine (any of a design's up to eight), or the leg;
  - **when:** now; at a moment (on the take-off roll above 100 kt, at rotation, after lift-off, climbing through 10,000 ft, in cruise, on approach below 3,000 ft, on short final below 500 ft); after a time; or at random within a window, one of the ticked kinds, its time and choice drawn from a seed when it is armed.
  - **V1:** the game shows none. The take-off roll above 100 kt stands for it, unless you add a V1 from the type's numbers (`[EST]`, logged).
- **The plan** (what is armed) goes in the snapshot beside the weather (`snap.failures`), kept by a rewind and a jump as the weather is. **The state** (which have fired, a fire burning, the bottles used, each leg's position) goes in the core, restored by them.
- **The predictor** gets the plan without the hidden random failures. A jump whose target lies past a hidden failure's moment ends at the last sample before it.
- **Repair:** one failure, or all; the aircraft as repaired (an engine then starts by its switch; the gear goes where its lever says).
- **With no failure armed, the sim is v0.7.9's to the bit:** the golden flights exact, and an old save loading as before (no `failures`: none armed).

**Check (node):**
- each kind set at a time: a run with a snapshot taken and restored in the middle equals a straight run, sample for sample;
- a rewind to before a fired failure fires it again at its moment;
- a save's JSON round-trips the plan and the state;
- a hidden failure is absent from the predictor's snapshot, and a jump past its moment ends before it;
- the golden flights and `scenario-state` exact; an old save loads with no failures.

### 3. The warnings and the checklists

**From the code:**
- **The voice's warnings** (`audio/callouts.js`: pull up, too low gear, sink rate, windshear) use the browser's speech. The PFD's SILENCE button and the `'` key silence what is sounding (`audio/silence.js`, v0.7.4).
- **The engine panel** (`ui/engines-panel.js`, the A321's) and **the EICAS** (`ui/boeing/eicas.js`) are Canvas 2D, s × 0.62 s, redrawn at most 30 times a second; both draw a design's engines in one row (`engine-row.js`). The EICAS lists "ENG n OFF" in amber under the systems; both show a white memo for a design's dropped tanks. Neither takes clicks today. The HUD's buttons keep the keyboard on the flight (`mousedown` → `preventDefault()`, `hud.js`).
- **The synth** (`audio/synth.js`) is Web Audio. The repository has no recorded sounds (the public-repository rule).

**Make:**
- **Master warning and master caution:** a red and an amber light by the PFD (Airbus's MASTER WARN and MASTER CAUT; the Boeings' WARNING and CAUTION). A click cancels the aural and the light, as in the aircraft; the SILENCE button and `'` cancel them too.
- **The aurals, synthesised:** Airbus's continuous repetitive chime for a red warning and single chime for an amber caution; the Boeings' fire bell, siren and caution beeper. Which sound goes with which warning from the public FCOM-level sources the run can reach.
- **The messages:**
  - the A321's in the engine panel's lower part, as ECAM's: the title in red or amber, the actions in cyan;
  - the Boeings' on the EICAS as a list (warnings red, cautions amber), above the "ENG n OFF" lines.
- **The checklist:** for each failure, the real procedure in short form.
  - Where a step is a switch, it is clickable and does the action: ENG MASTER OFF, FIRE PUSH, AGENT 1 DISCH, the gravity extension.
  - Where a step is flying (THR LEVER IDLE, LAND ASAP), the game ticks it when it is true.
  - It is a DOM panel by the engine display, in the screens' font (B612), that keeps the keyboard on the flight; it opens with its failure and can be folded away.
  - The steps' order and words come from the public FCOM-level sources the run can reach, `[EST]` where a type's own can't be read.

**Check:**
- **Node:** each failure raises its warning, light, aural and message, and clears with its repair; a click on a light cancels it; the checklist's steps in order, each switch step doing its action.
- **Chrome:** the lights, the messages and a checklist on the A321 and on a Boeing, at 1440×900 and 1280×720, fitting the panels (as `browser-weather-tab.mjs` checks the Weather tab).

### 5. The engines (Shane's decision for the switch)

**From the code:**
- **`engines.js`'s `engineStep(e, …)`:** `e.fuelOk` false windmills; there is no seizure, fire or relight today.
- **Per-engine levers** since v0.5.6 (`controls.thrustEng`); the laws leave a thrust asymmetry to the pilot (v0.5.8).

**Make:**
- **The switch:** the A321's ENG MASTER n (ON, OFF) and the Boeings' FUEL CONTROL n (RUN, CUTOFF), drawn under each engine's gauges on the engine panel and the EICAS; a design's under each of its engines. A click cuts the engine off; another starts it.

- **Seizure:** N1 to 0 within seconds, no windmilling, more drag; no relight.
- **Fire:** the red warning and its aural; the engine runs until it is shut down; the checklist (the lever to idle, the switch off, fire push, agent 1, agent 2 if still burning after 30 s, land at the nearest suitable airport); the odds seeded per failure; a fire not put out stays a warning.

### 6. The gear

**From the code:**
- **`sim.js`:** one `X.gearPos` (0 up, 1 down) and `X.gearDoors` for all three legs (`updateGear()`: the doors, then the travel, over `A.GEAR_DOOR_S` and `A.GEAR_TRAVEL_S`). The legs (`mkLegs()`: nose, left, right) touch the ground only when `gearPos > 0.98`.
- **Without Indestructible,** the fuselage, the tail, an engine or a wingtip touching the ground is a crash (`CRASH_POINTS`). With it, the crash points and three belly points are skids, springs that push back (`SKID_POINTS`, v0.4.1 and v0.4.2), and the landing card knows a gear-up landing (`score.js`).
- **The GPWS's "too low, gear"** exists.

**Make:**
- **Each leg its own position.** `X.gearPos` reads as before where all three agree, so a flight with no failure is v0.7.9's to the bit.
- **Won't extend:** the doors open and the legs stay up. The checklist's gravity extension (the A321's crank, the Boeings' ALTN GEAR) drops them by gravity in about 30 s (`[EST]`); it can fail too, if armed.
- **A leg that won't lock:** that leg stays up whatever is done.
- **A leg that collapses:** on the next landing, at the touchdown or on the roll.
- **A landing with the gear up or a leg missing,** within limits (the sink rate, the bank and the pitch: the gear's 10 ft/s and the skids' margins, `[EST]`), slides to a stop on the skids, the nacelles and the belly scraping; the landing card says a gear-up landing. Past the limits it is a crash, as today. Any sparks are a look behind a switch.

**Check:**
- **Node:** each leg's position; the gravity extension; landings on two legs and on none at Gimpo's 14R, the aircraft stopping on the runway under the limit and crashing over it; the golden flights exact.
- **Chrome:** a gear-up landing at Heathrow and a landing on two legs at Incheon, with pictures.
