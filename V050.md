## Shane's decisions (2026-11-13)

Claude proposed these as defaults (`HANDOFF.md`, "Proposed, not confirmed"), and Shane picked them, with his own deadline.

1. **The scope: engines, weight and fuel.** The warehouse changes:
   - the engine count: 2, 3, 4 or 6;
   - where they hang on each wing;
   - which engine: the LEAP-1A, the GEnx-1B or the GE90-115B (the three the game already has);
   - a thrust scale;
   - the empty weight and the fuel capacity.

   Not the wing, the fuselage or the tail: those come later (the aerodynamics from the shape is its own step).
2. **Where it lives: a page on the main menu.** Warehouse on the main menu. The aircraft stands in 3D, to orbit by dragging as in 3rd person, with the parts beside it and the numbers that follow from them: thrust-to-weight, MTOW, range, each engine's ground clearance. Saved designs appear in New scenario's aircraft picker, beside the three types.
3. **Realistic limits.**
   - **The pylons:** a pylon holds about 1.5 times its engine's rated thrust (CS-25's ultimate factor). Past it the warehouse warns, and in flight the engine tears off: it stops and it's gone (the debris and the crash physics come in v0.12.0).
   - **The ground:** a big engine on a small aircraft (a GE90 on the A321) scrapes, as it would in reality, unless you lengthen the gear in the warehouse. The tail-strike angle follows the gear.
4. **The deadline: 08:00 KST, Friday 13 November 2026.**

**HANDOFF's other defaults, taken as they were** (Shane wasn't asked; change nothing here without a reason, and log it if you do):

- **A design's base** is always one of the three types. A design changes parts on it; its name and registration are yours to type.
- **The screens show every engine:** the A321's engine panel and the Boeings' EICAS draw as many as there are. **The sound:** each engine its own voice in the synth, up to six; if time runs short, voices by side with the level of their engines is enough for v0.5.0 (log it).
- **Who builds it:** this run.

**Decided in the Cowork session** (where Shane's words leave a choice):

- **Each engine brings its own pylon,** rated for 1.5 times its own engine type's rated take-off thrust. So a GE90 on an A321 hangs on a GE90's pylon and holds at 100 % (Shane's own example from 2026-11-01 works), and it's the thrust scale that can pull an engine off: past 1.5, the engine tears off once its thrust passes the pylon's limit.
- **Engines on the wings come in symmetric pairs:** 2, 4 or 6, each pair at its own spanwise station. **Three engines** are a pair plus one on the centreline in the tail, DC-10 style, through the fin's root: it's the only engine off the wing, so build it last, and if time runs out leave 3 out of the warehouse and log it.
- **The structural limits stay the base type's:** MTOW, MLW and MZFW don't change. Heavier engines and more fuel eat into the payload, as they would.

## The rules every part keeps

- **The three types don't change.** Built from parts, the A321neo, the 787-9 and the 777-300ER fly exactly as v0.4.4 does:
  - the golden A321 flight (`tests/golden-a321.test.mjs`) matches to 1e-9, and so do the two Boeings' golden flights, recorded first (Part 1);
  - all 674 unit tests pass with their assertions unchanged (imports may move).

  Keep the arithmetic the same for the base types, not just nearly the same numbers: where two engines were added as `thrL + thrR`, a loop over two engines must add them in the same order. A base type's engines are already inside its OEW, CG and inertias, so only a design's differences from its base (an engine added, moved or swapped) change the mass properties.
- **A design is data.** One plain object, for example `{ id, name, registration, base, engines: { type, count, stations }, thrustScale, oewDeltaKg, fuelCapacityKg, gearExtendM }` (the shape is yours; log it). Everything reads the aircraft it flies from it: the sim, the prediction worker, the model, the panels, the synth, the score and the logbook.
- **Saves carry the design whole.** `sim.snapshot()` and a scenario embed the design itself, not just its id, so a design deleted or edited later doesn't change a saved flight. A save from before v0.5.0 restores as its base type, exactly as it flew.
- **Seeded and deterministic:** the prediction worker steps at 1/30 s and the live sim at 1/120 s, and a jump restores a worker snapshot, so anything a design adds to the sim (a pylon giving way, for one) is a function of the state, not of `Math.random()`.
- **Storage:** designs under `gamedump:flight-sim:designs`. Every key starts `gamedump:flight-sim:`.
- **The CPU rules at runtime** (`PLAN.md` → CPU at runtime): 60 fps at 1440×900 on the M5 with six engines; the warehouse's 3D view renders only while its page is open, and nothing runs while paused or hidden.
- **No new keys,** and no Ctrl bindings (Cmd/Ctrl+L stays the only one).

## Part 1: golden flights, then the engines as a list (first, and alone)

1. **Before changing anything, record the Boeings' golden flights** from v0.4.4's pushed code, with the A321's recorder (`tests/golden-a321-flight.mjs`), made to take the type: the same scripted flight (RKSS 14R, the take-off, the climb on the autopilot and 10 minutes on), the state every second, kept as `tests/fixtures/golden-b787-9.json.gz` and `golden-b777-300er.json.gz`, with a test for each like `golden-a321.test.mjs`. They record the Boeings as they are, late lift-off included (`ROADMAP.md`, "Found while building v0.4.4"): that's intended. Never re-record a golden to make a test pass.
2. **The engines as a list in the sim's core.** Today there are two by name (`n1L`, `n1R`, `revL`, `revR`, `thrL`, `thrR`, `ffL`, `ffR`, `n1tL`, `n1tR`): each engine gets its own state (N1, its target, thrust, fuel flow, reverser), stepped in a loop. The thrust's moments already loop over `ENGINES`; `trimAirborne()` splits the thrust by two at `ENGINES[0].z`, and must split it over the list. `state.engines` reports every engine.
3. **The thrust levers, the A/THR and the reversers** command every engine.
4. **Saves:** the snapshot carries the list; a v0.4.4 snapshot (`n1L` and the rest) restores into it.

**Done when** the three golden flights match to 1e-9, the 674 old tests pass unchanged, and a snapshot round trip holds. Checkpoint in `STATUS.md`. Parts 2 to 5 can then go to subagents in parallel, once the design's data shape (Part 2's first step) is fixed.

## Part 2: designs (the data, the sim, the prediction, saves)

1. **The engine types** as data: for the LEAP-1A, the GEnx-1B and the GE90-115B, the numbers their types use today (thrust, idle, spool, TSFC, N1 at TOGA, the reversers: from `sim/a321neo.js`, `sim/b787-9.js` and `sim/b777-300er.js`), plus each one's **dry weight** from its engine type certificate (EASA or FAA TCDS, with the source, as the data modules do; [EST] with the reasoning if not found), its nacelle's size (from the models) and its voice (`audio/engine-sounds.js` has the GEnx-1B and the GE90-115B; the LEAP-1A's is `synth.js`'s default).
2. **A design becomes a kit** like a type's (`kitFor()` in `sim/types.js`): its engines' positions (thrust points and nacelles), its mass, CG and inertias (each engine's weight at its position, against the base's), its fuel capacity (extra fuel in the centre tank: log your choice), its gear (lengthened by `gearExtendM`), its crash points and Indestructible's skids (each nacelle has its own, as today's two do), and its limits.
3. **The ranges:**
   - stations: along each wing between the fuselage side plus a nacelle's width and about 70 % of the semi-span, clear of each other and of the main gear;
   - the thrust scale: 0.5 to 3.0;
   - the empty weight: −15 % to +30 % of the base's OEW;
   - the fuel capacity: half to twice the base's;
   - the gear: 0 to 1.5 m longer (main and nose together).
4. **The prediction worker and the jumps** get the design, as they get the type. A scenario carries its design, and the logbook tags the flight with the design's name.

**Tests:** a design's snapshot round trip; the prediction agreeing with the live sim for a design as well as for its base; asymmetric thrust (one engine of a pair at idle) yawing and rolling by the sum of the engines' moments; a four-engine 777's mass, CG and inertias against a hand sum.

## Part 3: the model from the design

1. **One engine builder for all three aircraft:** each engine type's nacelle (fan, fan case, spinner, cascades, core and plug, as `widebody.js` builds them for the Boeings and `aircraft.js` for the A321) and its pylon, hung at its station, so a GE90 can hang on an A321 and four can hang on a 777. Each fan spins with its own engine's N1.
2. **On the Boeings, each engine's parts pass their own pylon span as `bendY`** (the Builder's fifth argument, v0.4.3), so each engine rides the bent wing as one piece and its fan stays centred.
3. **The tail engine** (three engines), DC-10 style, through the fin's root.
4. **Longer gear:** the legs grow below the wing (the main legs' pivots stay inside the wing, `mainPivotZ` since v0.4.4), the wheels sit lower, and `GEAR`, `GROUND_Z`, the crash points and the pilots' eye height all follow.
5. **The registration** on the livery is the design's.

Look at it: a four-engine 777, a GE90 A321 with its gear lengthened, and a six-engine 787, from the side, from below and in flight with the wing bent (`tests/cloud/look.cjs` shows any view of a type on its dev page; teach it a design).

## Part 4: the warehouse page

1. **Warehouse on the main menu,** after Load scenario: a list of designs (New from the A321neo, the 787-9 or the 777-300ER; Duplicate, Rename, Delete), and the editor.
2. **The editor:**
   - the aircraft in 3D on its own canvas, orbited by dragging and zoomed by pinching or scrolling, as in 3rd person;
   - beside it, the parts: the engine count, the engine type, each pair's station, the thrust scale, the empty weight, the fuel capacity, the gear, the name and the registration;
   - the numbers, live: the OEW, MTOW (the base's), the fuel capacity, thrust-to-weight at MTOW, a range (from the setup's fuel estimate), each engine's ground clearance at rest and at the tail-strike attitude, each pylon's load at full thrust against its limit, and the tail-strike angle;
   - warnings in amber, and red where something will fail (a pylon) or touch (a nacelle).
3. **Saved designs in New scenario's aircraft picker,** under the three types: a card with the design's name, its base and a line such as "4 × GE90-115B".
4. **It renders only while open,** and leaves nothing running when you go back.

## Part 5: the limits in flight

1. **A pylon giving way:** when an engine's thrust passes its pylon's limit (1.5 times its type's rated take-off thrust), the engine tears off: its thrust and fuel flow stop, its mass leaves (the CG and the inertias follow), the model hides it and its pylon, and a message says so. Deterministic, so the prediction agrees with it. Indestructible doesn't stop it: it's a failure, not a crash (log it).
2. **A nacelle on the ground:** each nacelle's crash point strikes as today's do (or slides as a skid with Indestructible on).
3. **Off-centre thrust** yaws and rolls by the engines' moments (the moments loop), with an engine gone, at idle or reversing alone.
