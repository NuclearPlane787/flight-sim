## Part 1: the main menu and scenarios

1. **A main menu, shown on load** instead of the flight setup.
   - **Layout:** like Satisfactory's title screen, with the title and version on the left, the buttons on the right, and a gradient over a still. Read `games/satisfactory/index.html` (`#start`), its `style.css` (`.screen--title`), `src/icons.js` (`pickBackdrop()`) and `src/ui.js` (where it's used). Read them only; change nothing in Satisfactory.
   - **Buttons:**
     - Continue: the newest scenario, under its name and where the aircraft is. Hidden when there's none.
     - New scenario, Load scenario, Logbook, Settings, Controls.
     - Exit to Game Dump (`../../index.html`).
   - **The still:** 2–4 JPGs rendered from the game in Chrome on this Mac's GPU, in `assets/menu/`, at most about 400 KB each, one picked at random per visit as `pickBackdrop()` does. For example: the A321neo at a Gimpo gate, on final to Jeju 07 with Hallasan behind, or over Seoul. One can be at night once Part 2 is done. They're rendered from the game, so the public repository can carry them.
   - **CPU:** nothing renders or steps behind the menu (PLAN.md → CPU at runtime). The still is the backdrop.
2. **Scenarios: save slots with the whole state.**
   - **What a save holds:** HANDOFF.md → "What a save has to hold" lists it. In short:
     - `sim.snapshot()`;
     - the plan;
     - the gate flow, including a pushback under way and the taxi route;
     - `landed`, `arrivalStand`, `liftoffT`, `fuelAtStart` and `input.lever`;
     - the scorer, with its approach track;
     - the camera's view and looks;
     - **the clock** (time, date, running, speed): a `clock` field from the start, fixed at a day time until Part 2 makes it run.
     Not saved: time compression (back to 1×), the rewind ring, and the callouts' armed state.
   - **Exactly where it was:** a loaded scenario is where it was saved, in position, speed, attitude, autopilot and FCU, fuel and sim time, and it flies on the same way.
   - **The store:** localStorage. An index, `gamedump:flight-sim:scenarios` (id, name, created, savedAt, a summary), and one key per scenario, `gamedump:flight-sim:scenario:<id>`.
     - Model it on Satisfactory's `src/saves.js`: a class over a Storage-like object, so the tests can use a Map.
     - Every Game Dump game shares the origin's ~5 MB. Keep saves small (round what doesn't need full precision). A refused write shows a toast, and the game goes on.
   - **Autosave, as in Satisfactory:**
     - every 60 s in flight, on pause, when the tab hides or the page closes (`pagehide`), and on Exit to menu;
     - also Save in the pause menu;
     - never a crashed state: a crash leaves the last good save.
   - **Names:** 3–40 characters, "Scenario N" by default (Satisfactory's `cleanName`/`nameOk`).
   - **Load scenario:** newest first, each with:
     - its name and route;
     - where the aircraft is ("FL350, 120 nm to RKPC" or "Parked at Jeju stand 12");
     - when it was saved.
     Rename and delete; delete asks first.
   - **New scenario:** a name field on the existing flight setup screen (`src/ui/setup.js`), then Fly.
   - **New flight inside a scenario** (pause menu): it replaces that scenario's flight. Parked at a stand after arriving, the setup starts from that airport and stand.
   - **Migration from v0.1.4:** with no scenarios yet and a `lastflight`, make "Scenario 1" from it. It starts at its route's start, since v0.1.x never saved the aircraft's state.
     - Don't delete or rewrite v0.1.4's keys in a way that loses anything: Shane's real logbook is in them.
3. **One logbook:** `gamedump:flight-sim:logbook`.
   - New entries carry the scenario's id and name, and entries without one count as Scenario 1's.
   - The pause menu's Logbook shows this scenario's flights; the main menu's shows all of them, with a filter by scenario.
   - Deleting a scenario keeps its flights, under the name it had.
4. **Settings and Controls** pages, from the main menu and the pause menu.
   - Settings: the master volume, the callouts, the performance readout (and the Sky window's button, in Part 2).
   - Controls: the keys table, with the FCU and map notes.
5. **The pause menu:** Resume, Save, Logbook, Rewind 30 s, Restart the flight, New flight, Settings, Controls, and Exit to menu (which saves first). Keep v0.1.4's look: Barlow, the dark buttons, the primary button in cyan.

## Part 2: night (once Part 1 is done and verified)

In this order. Each item leaves the game working, and the reviewer checks against this list.

1. **The clock and the Sky window.**
   - **The clock:** the time of day is a clock in KST, with a date. It runs with the sim, so time compression speeds it up too.
   - **The Sky window,** like Satisfactory's (read it in `games/satisfactory/`: `src/ui.js`, `src/daycycle.js`, and the `.sky-*` styles in its `style.css`):
     - **Opening it:** L, and Cmd+L or Ctrl+L as in Satisfactory, where Shane uses it, with the browser's default prevented, as Satisfactory does (`src/input.js`, and its `KeyL` handler in `src/main.js`). Also from the pause menu and Settings. L or Esc closes it. Typing in a text field (a scenario's name) never opens it.
     - **The one Ctrl binding:** PLAN.md says no Ctrl keys, because Ctrl+W closes the tab on Windows. Cmd/Ctrl+L can't lose anything (if a browser keeps it, its address bar takes the focus), so it's the exception. Note it in PLAN.md.
     - **Placement:** it stands aside, so the sky shows, and the flight goes on with its keys working.
     - **In it:**
       - a little sky with the sun's and the Moon's paths for that day at the aircraft's position, and the Moon's phase;
       - a 24-hour track to click or drag;
       - play and pause;
       - the clock's speed (− and +, where 1× is real time with the sim);
       - jumps to sunrise, noon, sunset, midnight and now (the real time in Korea);
       - the date, with a day back, a day forward and Today.
   - **The flight setup** has the start time and date: Now (the real time in Korea, as MSFS defaults to) or one picked. Scenarios save the clock, and a migrated Scenario 1 starts in the afternoon.
   - **The sun and the Moon:** placed from the date, the time and the aircraft's latitude and longitude, with the standard low-precision formulas. Sunrise and sunset should be within a couple of minutes, and the Moon's phase comes from its elongation.
   - **The light follows the sun:** `uSunDir`, `uSunColor` and `uSkyAmb` (the world uniforms in `src/world/glsl.js`), the haze (`ATMOS_GLSL` and `applyHaze` in `src/world/sky.js`), and the `DirectionalLight`, `HemisphereLight` and `scene.fog` that `createSky()` adds for three's own materials (the aircraft). With the sun where v0.1.4 fixed it (230°, 35° up), the day looks as it does now.
     - It runs through day, golden hour, and civil, nautical and astronomical twilight to night, lit by the Moon (its phase sets how much) and the sky's glow.
     - The terrain, sea, buildings, aircraft, runways and cockpit all take it. Nothing goes pitch black.
2. **The night sky** (`src/world/sky.js`): Earth's, in Satisfactory's look.
   - **Sources:** read Satisfactory's `src/sky.js`, and copy what's useful into Flight Sim's own files; never import across games.
   - **The stars:** a dense field, with a few hundred of the brightest real stars where they really are, so the constellations are right. Use a public-domain catalogue or public positions, and credit it.
   - **The Milky Way,** along the galactic plane. Both it and the stars turn with sidereal time.
   - **The Moon:** at its position and phase, with a glow.
   - **Twilight:** colours at sunset and sunrise, and the stars fading in as the sun goes below −6°.
3. **City lights** (Shane: "the city gaining point-source lights like looking out from a plane window"):
   - **Where:** wherever the land cover is urban (`hf.coverAt(x, y).urban`, the cover texture's blue channel in the shaders), procedural and seeded like the buildings in `src/world/cities.js`.
   - **What:**
     - streetlights along a street grid, in warm sodium orange and white LED;
     - brighter commercial cores;
     - lit apartment windows close up.
   - **From cruise:** Seoul and the coast are a sea of points, thinning with distance. Each point stays at least about a pixel, with a slight sparkle.
   - **Cost:** generated on the GPU or in instanced batches, so a few draw calls, not one per light. Off by day.
4. **Airports at night** (Shane: "with runways appropriate lit up"):
   - **The runway, approach and PAPI lights:** already placed (`lightLayout` in `src/world/airports.js`, drawn by `LIGHT_VERT`/`LIGHT_FRAG`). Make them bright with halos at night, dimmer by day, and readable from 15 nm. They are:
     - edge lights, white turning yellow in the last 600 m;
     - centreline lights, white, then alternating, then red;
     - threshold lights green, end lights red;
     - the touchdown zone lights;
     - the approach lights with the rabbit;
     - the PAPI.
   - **Taxiways:** green centreline lights and blue edge lights along the taxiways, and red stop bars at the runway holding points.
   - **The aprons:** floodlights (a warm pool on the pavement), and lit terminal windows.
5. **The A321's own lights** (`src/aircraft.js`), set automatically by phase, as pilots use them:
   - red and green nav lights and the white tail light;
   - the red beacons on top and bottom while the engines run;
   - white strobes on the runway and in the air;
   - landing and taxi lights, with pools of light on the ground (the ground's shaders are the game's own, so three's lights don't reach them: pass the beams in as uniforms);
   - the logo light on the tail.
   The parked aircraft show their nav lights.
6. **The cockpit at night:** dim panel lighting, with the screens readable.
