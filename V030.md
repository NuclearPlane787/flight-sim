## Claude's defaults

Claude chose these on 2026-11-04 and told Shane. If he changes one in the starting prompt, his word wins.

1. **The variants:** the **787-9 with GEnx-1B engines** and the **777-300ER with GE90-115Bs**: the most-built variant of each, with one engine option each.
2. **They fly like Boeings, not like an A321 in a Boeing's skin.** `ROADMAP.md` row 3: "the 777 and 787 have Boeing's fly-by-wire, which a pilot can override." What the player should feel:
   - **Speed-stable pitch (Boeing's C\*U law):** the aircraft holds the speed it's trimmed for. New pitch-trim keys move that speed; let go of the controls and it settles back toward it; thrust changes pitch. There's no auto-trim holding the flight path, as the A321's Normal law has. With the autopilot on, it trims itself.
   - **Soft protections:** stall protection (the trim stops at the minimum manoeuvring speed; the pitch-limit indicator on the PFD; the stick shaker), overspeed protection (a nose-up push; the trim stops at VMO/MMO) and bank-angle protection (past 35° it rolls back to 30° once released). Holding full input overrides each, as force overrides the real ones. There's no alpha floor: find out what the 777's and 787's autothrottles do instead at low speed, and do that.
   - **Boeing's flap detents,** from each type's documents, not the Airbus's 1/1+F/2/3/FULL.
   - **Boeing's autothrottle and MCP:** the MCP in place of the FCU (with the FCU's typing and scrolling), Boeing's modes (THR REF, SPD, HOLD, IDLE; FLCH, V/S, ALT HOLD, HDG SEL, LNAV and VNAV where the A321 has managed modes, LOC, G/S, APP) and Boeing's FMA on the PFD. The guidance underneath can stay the A321's. Since v0.2.2, R or F turns the A321's A/THR off. Find out how a pilot overrides a 777's or 787's autothrottle with the levers, and do the same.
   - **Boeing-style PFD, ND and EICAS** for the two Boeings. The A321 keeps its own.
3. **The 787-9 first** (Part 2), **then the 777-300ER** (Part 3), which reuses the 787's Boeing systems.
4. **The livery and registrations:** Nuclear Airlines on both, fitted to each shape. Each gets a new HL registration (HL8787 stays the A321's). Log the choice.
5. **The picker:** on the setup screen (New scenario), with each type's picture (rendered from the game), its name and a few key numbers. A scenario keeps its type, and saves from before v0.3.0 load as the A321neo. The logbook shows each flight's type.

## Part 1: aircraft types, with the A321neo unchanged

1. **Before changing anything, record a golden A321 flight:** a scripted run of the sim (RKSS 14R: the take-off, the climb on the autopilot and 10 minutes on) with its state every second, kept as a test fixture.
2. **A type registry.** Each type is one data module with every number and its source, as `src/sim/a321neo.js` is ([TCDS], [AC], [EST] with the reasoning). Everything that reads `a321neo.js` today reads the active type instead: the sim, the FBW, the autopilot, the engines, the synth, the model and livery, the cockpit, the panels, the score, the setup screen, the gate flow and the camera's eye points.
   - **The prediction worker** (`src/sim/predict*.js`) runs the sim too, so it must get the same type.
   - **Saves:** `sim.snapshot()`/`restore()` and the scenario carry the type. A save without one is the A321neo.
3. **The A321neo doesn't change:** the golden flight matches to 1e-9, and the 279 unit tests pass with their assertions unchanged (imports may move).
4. **The picker** (one entry until Part 2 is done), the scenario's type and the logbook's.
5. **Stands for widebodies:** the 787-9 (span about 60 m) and the 777-300ER (about 65 m) are ICAO code E; the A321neo (35.8 m) is code C. The gate flow gives a widebody a code E stand at each airport (`reference/notes/airports.md` and the stand data), keeps the parked traffic clear of it, and gives its longer wheelbase room on the pushback and the taxi. If an airport has no such stand in the data, use a remote stand and log it.

## Part 2: the 787-9 (once Part 1 is done and verified)

**Must** (these are in the definition of done):

1. **The numbers,** each with its source, in a new data module like `a321neo.js`:
   - the type certificate (FAA TCDS T00021SE, or EASA's validation TCDS, which downloads as easily as the A321's did) for the speeds, limits, weights, fuel and CG;
   - Boeing's *787 Airplane Characteristics for Airport Planning* (D6-58333) for the dimensions, weights, ground clearances, the pilots' eye point, turning, and the take-off and landing field-length charts;
   - the GEnx-1B's engine TCDS for its rating and shaft speeds (the sound needs them);
   - PDFs go in `reference/docs/` (git-ignored by `*.pdf`), with their download commands added to `PLAN.md` → Data. **Download honestly:** a real user agent, no faking a browser. If a site refuses three times, stop, put the manual route in STATUS.md for Shane, and work from public figures with their URLs meanwhile;
   - what the documents don't give (aerodynamic coefficients, inertias) is [EST] with the reasoning, checked against the documents' performance. Write the checks up in `reference/notes/b787-9-performance.md`, as `a321neo-performance.md` does.
2. **The flight model** (aero; engines with their spool, idle and reversers; fuel; weight and CG; inertias; gear with 4-wheel main bogies), passing tests against the documents: the take-off field length at MTOW and the landing field length at MLW (sea level, ISA), the approach speed at MLW, and the climb and the cruise (M0.85) at typical weights. The tolerances and their reasoning go in the tests.
3. **Boeing's fly-by-wire and the trim keys** (default 2), with tests:
   - trimmed at 250 kt and released, it settles back to 250 kt;
   - the trim moves the reference speed;
   - past 35° of bank, it rolls back to 30°;
   - it won't trim below the minimum manoeuvring speed;
   - the overspeed push;
   - the flare takes back pressure.

   No Ctrl bindings (Cmd/Ctrl+L stays the only one). Update the Controls screen and the README.
4. **The exterior,** in `src/aircraft.js`'s style and within its budgets: the fuselage, the nose with its four cockpit windows, the raked wing, the GEnx nacelles with their chevrons, the main bogies, the tail, the lights by phase (v0.2.0's) and the livery. Compare the proportions with the planning document's three-view drawings (which stay out of the repository).
5. **The cockpit,** in the style of `src/cockpit.js` and `src/cockpit-windows.js`: the 787's four big windows, the glareshield with the MCP, the yokes, and both seats at the document's eye points. The view out over the nose matters most.
6. **The sound:** `src/audio/synth.js` reads each type's engine (fan blades, N1 and N2 at 100%, where the buzz-saw starts, the mix) in place of the LEAP-1A's constants at its top. `STATUS.md`'s v0.2.2 entry explains each voice. `tests/audio-render.mjs` passes for each type.
7. **The gate flow, the score and the camera** for the new type: its code E stand, its pushback and taxi, the tail-strike pitch from its geometry in the score, and a 3rd-person distance for its size.

**Should** (if time allows; otherwise log it for later):

8. **The MCP, the autothrottle and the FMA,** as default 2. If time runs short, the FCU with Boeing's names is the fallback; log it.
9. **Boeing-style PFD, ND and EICAS.**

**Nice** (only with time to spare): the 787's head-up displays, and its wings bending up in flight.

## Part 3: the 777-300ER (once Part 2 is done and verified)

The same list, reusing Part 2's Boeing systems, with its own documents: FAA TCDS T00001SE (or EASA's), Boeing's *777-200/300 Airplane Characteristics for Airport Planning* (D6-58329) and the GE90-115B's engine TCDS. What differs:

- the 6-wheel main bogies, with the aft axles' steering;
- the raked wingtips;
- the GE90-115B's nacelle: its fan is 3.25 m across, against the LEAP-1A's 1.98 m;
- the six cockpit windows;
- the 777's flap detents;
- its length: the tail-strike margin, and the wider turns at the stands;
- its far higher weights.

If Part 3 isn't done by the deadline, the 777 stays out of the picker and STATUS.md says where it stands. Shane decides whether to push v0.3.0 with the 787 alone or give the run a second night.
