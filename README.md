# Flight Sim

Airliners (the A321neo, the Boeing 787-9 and the 777-300ER) in two regions, over real coastline, elevation and land cover with procedural fine detail:

- **East Asia** (six more in v0.7.5): twenty-nine airports in Korea, Japan, China, North Korea and Russia (Incheon, Gimpo, Jeju, Gimhae, Cheongju and Kunsan; Tokyo Haneda and Narita, Osaka Kansai and Itami, Nagoya Centrair, Fukuoka, Hiroshima, Komatsu, Niigata, Sendai, Aomori and Sapporo New Chitose; Beijing Capital and Daxing, Shanghai Pudong and Hongqiao, Yancheng, Qingdao, Dalian and Shenyang; Pyongyang and Wonsan; Vladivostok);
- **Europe** (v0.7.0; nine more in v0.7.1, nine more in v0.7.2, eight more in v0.7.3, six military bases in v0.7.6): forty-three airports in the United Kingdom, Ireland, France, Germany, Austria, Spain, Portugal, Switzerland, the Netherlands, Belgium, Denmark, Italy, Czechia and Gibraltar (London Heathrow and Gatwick, Manchester, Edinburgh, Glasgow, Dublin, Paris Charles de Gaulle and Orly, Toulouse, Nice, Nantes, Lyon, Marseille, Frankfurt, Munich, Berlin, Düsseldorf, Cologne Bonn, Hamburg, Vienna, Innsbruck, Prague, Madrid, Barcelona, Santiago de Compostela, Lisbon, Porto, Zurich, Geneva, Amsterdam Schiphol, Brussels, Copenhagen, Rome Fiumicino, Milan Malpensa, Venice, Naples, Gibraltar; and the air bases Ramstein, Lossiemouth, Lakenheath, Fairford, Zaragoza and Rota).

Seventy-two airports in all.

Flights stay inside a region. It has:

- realistic physics with each maker's fly-by-wire: Airbus's protections on the A321neo, Boeing's speed-stable law and manual trim on the 787 and 777 (or, with Boeing auto-trim on in Settings, a path that holds itself as the A321's does);
- a holds-only autopilot with up to 16× time compression;
- a path map that lets you jump ahead along the predicted path, change the route in flight, and skip to a 15 nm final; hover over the magenta route for the heading that follows it (v0.6.2); the cities show on it as pale areas with their names in English, more of them as you zoom in (v0.6.3); since v0.7.4 the land is coloured by height with a soft hillshade and a legend in feet, the countries' borders are a warm grey dash-dot line with their names fading in as you zoom out, the 250 m data's edge is clearer and its staircases cut diagonally, and zoomed in to an airport its terminals are drawn, the passenger terminals with their numbers; since v0.7.9 the military bases are amber and the airports shared with an air base carry an amber diamond, an airport zoomed in is drawn in full (aprons and taxiways at their widths, the taxiways' names, hold bars, runway numbers, centrelines and stand numbers, down to 1 m a pixel), and your assigned taxi route is drawn from the aircraft to its hold or stand (`?map79=0` for v0.7.8's map);
- gate starts with pushback and a taxi route drawn on the ground, and taxiway and runway signs (v0.4.1; runway exit and distance remaining signs, v0.4.2);
- parked aircraft and ground vehicles at the airports;
- synthesised engine sound built from each engine's real tones (LEAP-1A, GEnx-1B, GE90-115B: the fan's blade-passing tone, the buzz-saw, the core's whine, the jet's roar), muffled but clearly there in the cockpit, warning sounds (silenced by the SILENCE button or ', v0.7.4), the radio-altitude callouts (RETARD included), a volume for each kind of sound (v0.7.4), a landing score and a logbook.
- failures since v0.8.0 ("Failures" below): a Failures tab where an engine's flameout, seizure or fire, the gear's failures, the hydraulics' (a pump, a system, two systems, with the degraded control laws they bring) and jammed flaps or slats are armed for now, a moment of the flight, a time or at random; master warning and caution with their sounds, the messages on the engine display and a clickable checklist for each; an engine shut down and restarted by its switch on the engine display; landings on two legs or none that slide to a stop.

`Updates.md` lists what each version added; the game shows its version in the Esc menu.

## The regions (v0.7.0)

- **Choosing one:** New scenario asks for the region first (East Asia or Europe), then the airports. The game loads one region at a time: picking the other one reloads the page into it and opens its setup. The last region used is remembered; East Asia is the default. Load scenario lists the saves grouped by region, and loading one from the other region reloads into it and starts it there. Saves made before v0.7.0 are East Asia's. The home page's ticket and the logbook name either region's airports. The warehouse's Fly it uses the last region.
- **Each region is its own world,** in its own map frame: East Asia in Korea's (a Lambert conformal conic at 36° N 127.5° E), Europe in one at 47° N 4° E with its standard parallels at 39° and 54° N.
- **Europe's shape:** its edge is a polygon, not a rectangle: 35° to 59° N and 11° W to 19.5° E, cut diagonally in the south-east so that Algeria and Tunisia are out and Sicily is in, and in the south-west so that Morocco's coast along the Strait is in. The north takes in all of mainland Scotland, Denmark and Gothenburg; the east gives Vienna room and puts Budapest at the edge. The path map draws the edge, and the predicted path ends at it.
- **Europe's ground:** 250 m terrain over the 224 tiles of 128 km with land within 200 km of one of the airports (`data/europe/terrain/`; 93 in v0.7.0; v0.7.1's batch B of 77 adds Ireland, Wales and northern England, Denmark and the south of Sweden, Brandenburg, Austria east to Vienna and into Hungary, Portugal and the south-west of France; v0.7.2's batch C of 31 adds Scotland to the north-west and the Hebrides, Bohemia, Lake Geneva and the Alps to Mont Blanc, the Côte d'Azur, the Loire's estuary and Galicia; v0.7.3's batch D of 18 adds Andalusia and Morocco's north, the Strait, northern Portugal, Venice's plain and Campania, with a tile north of Hamburg; v0.7.6's batch E of 5 adds Lewis, Caithness and Orkney, Devon and the Bristol Channel, and the Gulf of Cádiz with the Algarve's east), 30 m land cover within 100 km of each, 30 m terrain at every airport, and 1 km land over the rest of the region, so no edge shows from cruise. The Netherlands' polders lie below sea level as they do (Schiphol's field at about −3 to −4 m), with the IJsselmeer and Markermeer as inland water. **Since v0.7.7 the lakes lie flat at their own level,** taken from the elevation model's own flattened surfaces (`tools/bake_region_lakes.py`), where every water cell had been drawn 2 m under its nearest bank: the IJsselmeer and the Markermeer at −0.25 m, Geneva 368.0 m, Constance 394.0 m, Garda 64.5 m, Lough Neagh 10.5 m, Loch Ness 16.0 m, Maggiore 193.0 m, Lucerne 433.5 m, Zurich 404.5 m, Bolsena 304.5 m; no water cell at the sea's 0; rivers, ponds and the water below a dam as before. Schiphol's 30 m ground has a floor at −7 m (it reached −19.5 m under Amsterdam's stadium), and Lisbon's keeps Cristo Rei's cliff (the ground under the statue 115 m, where the built-up filter had pulled it to 87 m).

### Europe's rules

- **Hectopascals and feet everywhere.** Shift+B sets the local QNH.
- **The transition altitude is the airport's own near it** (within 60 km; since v0.7.4 only on its own country's side, or a country its entry lists, as Nice lists Monaco) and the country's elsewhere: Heathrow and Gatwick 6,000 ft (the UK's 3,000 ft elsewhere), Charles de Gaulle and Orly 5,000 ft (the Paris TMA's), Frankfurt and Munich 5,000 ft, Madrid 13,000 ft, Barcelona 6,000 ft, Zurich 7,000 ft, Schiphol 3,000 ft and Fiumicino 6,000 ft (the last three estimates). Near Zurich it held across the German border until v0.7.4; now Germany's 5,000 ft holds on Germany's side, Switzerland's 7,000 ft at Lugano (not Malpensa's 6,000 ft), France's 5,000 ft on Geneva's French side and Italy's 6,000 ft east of Nice, and Gibraltar's own ground reads as Gibraltar, not Spain. v0.7.1: Austria 10,000 ft and Ireland 5,000 ft (each from its AIP's ENR 1.7); Denmark 3,000 ft, with Copenhagen's own 5,000 ft; Manchester's own 5,000 ft; Toulouse 5,000 ft (all read); Malpensa 6,000 ft, Belgium 4,500 ft and Portugal 4,000 ft are still estimates. v0.7.2: Czechia 5,000 ft (read: ENR 1.7), Düsseldorf, Cologne Bonn and Prague their countries' 5,000 ft (their AD 2.17 say the same), Nice and Nantes 5,000 ft (their TMAs'), Edinburgh, Glasgow and Santiago 6,000 ft (each from its AD 2.17), all read; Geneva 7,000 ft is an estimate. v0.7.3: Lyon and Marseille 5,000 ft (each from its AD 2.20), Hamburg Germany's 5,000 ft and Innsbruck Austria's 10,000 ft (its AD 2.17 says to take 10,000 ft), all read; Gibraltar 6,000 ft, Venice and Naples Italy's 6,000 ft and Porto Portugal's 4,000 ft are estimates. v0.7.6: Zaragoza and Rota 6,000 ft (each from its AD 2.17), read; Ramstein Germany's 5,000 ft and Lossiemouth, Lakenheath and Fairford the UK's 3,000 ft, their own not read. Ramstein's disc stops at the French border, about 50 km off.
- **Gibraltar's zone** is its own (`Europe/Gibraltar`, CET and CEST, hPa and feet, 6,000 ft). The game's country raster (2 km cells) reads Spain at the airport, which changes nothing a player sees: Spain's rules there are the same. The setup's group and the airport's clock go by the ICAO prefix (`LX`: Gibraltar).
- **Local time with summer time,** from the browser's own time-zone data: GMT and BST in the United Kingdom, GMT and IST in Ireland, WET and WEST in Portugal, CET and CEST in the rest (Gibraltar too). The clocks change on the last Sundays of March and October, and the labels follow the date. Morocco is on its own zone.
- **The magnetic variation** is the World Magnetic Model's (2025) everywhere in Europe.
- **The METAR line** in ICAO's standard form: `EGLL 00000KT CAVOK 15/07 Q1013 NOSIG`.
- **The setup** groups the airports by country (United Kingdom, France, Germany, Spain, Switzerland, the Netherlands, Italy; since v0.7.1 Ireland, Portugal, Belgium, Austria and Denmark too; since v0.7.2 Czechia; since v0.7.3 Gibraltar). The first flight it offers is Heathrow 27R to Charles de Gaulle 26L.

### Europe's cities

- **The towns** in five styles of their own, by country: Britain and Ireland (terraces of two-storey houses in parallel streets, semis, red and stock brick under slate, no towers); France (limestone under zinc grey, terracotta in the south); the centre (Germany, Austria, Switzerland: render under red-brown tile); the south (Spain, Portugal, Italy: cream and ochre under terracotta); the Low Countries and Denmark (brick under dark or red tile). Dense blocks are perimeter blocks of five to seven storeys round courtyards, with a tower only now and then; a church with a spire in some blocks.
- **The villages** in the same five groups: farmhouses and barns, houses of one to three storeys, and a church with a tower in most villages (since v0.7.4 the tower ends in a tall slate spire in the north and centre and a low tiled cap in Spain, Portugal and Italy, with a dark belfry opening and no window bands).
- **The fields by country** (v0.7.1), a whole district one kind: open fields of 5 to 12 ha with no hedges across northern France, Germany and the centre; small pastures of 1 to 2 ha inside dark hedges, with trees in about half of them, in Britain and Ireland, Normandy and Brittany to the Loire, and Spain's and Portugal's green north-west; dry fields in the south (pale fallow, ochre and red soil, olive groves as rows of dark crowns, terraces on slopes); and in the polders, the Fens and the marsh coasts, strips about 45 m by 600 m between dark ditches, parallel across about 8 km. No paddies. From far off, each zone's own average colour. ## Getting the data

Since v0.8.0 the baked world is a repository of its own, [flight-sim-data](https://github.com/NuclearPlane787/flight-sim-data), cloned as `data/` inside the game's folder (`games/flight-sim/` in Game Dump, or the root of a clone of this repository):

```sh
cd flight-sim
git clone https://github.com/NuclearPlane787/flight-sim-data.git data
```

This repository ignores `data/` (`.gitignore`). Its history still holds the data up to v0.7.9, so a checkout of v0.7.9 or earlier has its own. The data's commits carry the game's version numbers (`tools/push.sh` pushes both, the data first; a version that changed no data has no data commit). Without the data the loading screen says so in one line, with the clone command. The credits and licences of everything in it are in `data/README.md`.

## Running it

Serve GameDump's root and open the game:

```sh
cd GameDump
python3 -m http.server 8001
# http://localhost:8001/games/flight-sim/
```

It also runs from the hub's player (`play.html?game=flight-sim`). There's no build step: these are plain ES modules, three.js r186 is in `vendor/`, and the baked world is in `data/`. Chrome on a Mac is the target.

The game opens on the main menu, over a still rendered from the game: Continue (the newest scenario), New scenario, Load scenario, Logbook, Settings, Controls, and Exit to Game Dump.

**Scenarios** are save slots holding the whole state: the aircraft's position, speed and attitude, the autopilot and FCU, fuel, the gate flow (a pushback under way and the taxi route), the landing score's approach track, the camera and the clock. A loaded scenario is exactly where it was and flies on. They autosave every 60 s in flight, on pause, when the tab hides or closes, and on Exit to menu, and the Esc menu has Save. A crash never overwrites the last good save. Load scenario lists them newest first, with rename, duplicate and delete: a copy is the same moment, aircraft and load, so you can fly on from one point again and again (the Esc menu's Save a copy makes one of the present moment, and you fly on in the original). v0.1.x's last flight becomes "Scenario 1", with its logbook.

New scenario opens the flight setup with a name: the origin, destination, runways, load and fuel, the start (lined up on the runway, on a 15 nm final, or at a gate), and the time (Now, the real time at the departure, or a date and time picked in its local time: KST, JST or China's CST in East Asia; GMT or BST, CET or CEST and so on in Europe). New flight in the Esc menu replaces the scenario's flight, starting from the stand when parked after arriving. During a flight, the L window's Weight tab (or Weight and fuel in the Esc menu) changes the passengers, cargo and fuel, in the air or on the ground, with the setup's checks; Restart the flight goes back to the setup's load.

**Night:** the time of day runs with the sim, shown in local time where you are (time compression speeds it up), and the sun, the Moon (with its phase), the 8,400 brightest real stars (with fainter ones past them, washed out by a bright Moon) and the Milky Way are where they really are. On every clear night (v0.4.3) an aurora hangs low in the northern sky (in Europe since v0.7.4 only in the north: full over Scotland and Denmark, at 56° N and north of it, fading southwards and gone at 50° N; in East Asia since v0.7.6 full at 44° N and north of it, strong over Sapporo and Vladivostok, faint over Aomori and none at 40° N and south, so none over Sendai, Tokyo, Nagoya, Osaka or Seoul; `?auroraband=0` gives East Asia's old sky): green curtains with red tops and drifting rays, far off, behind any cloud; since v0.4.4 its folds open and close in slow waves and its shapes change as they go. The light runs through golden hour and twilight to a moonlit or starlit night, with the cities lit (streetlights, commercial cores, apartment windows; since v0.7.4 the terminals' glass lit storey by storey, with slab bands at each floor and stretches of dim offices, lit concourses and a few brighter bays, `?termnight=0` for the old pattern), the runways, approach lights, PAPIs, taxiways and aprons lit, and the A321's own lights set by phase as pilots use them. The Sky window (L) sets the time and date, the clock's speed and jumps to sunrise, noon, sunset, midnight or now. **Cities at night** (v0.7.9): the main roads are lit as lines of streetlights (motorways, trunk and primary roads from OpenStreetMap, inside the cities), cars run on the motorways (headlights toward you, tail lights away, thinning late at night), the downtowns are brighter, parks and water stay dark, and the colours follow the country; `?lights79=0` gives v0.7.8's city lights.

**Weather** (v0.4.0): the L window has a third tab, Weather (Sky · Weight · Weather). Pick a preset (clear, few clouds, overcast, rain, a light, moderate or severe storm, snow, fog, gusty crosswind), or change any setting under it: the wind by height, gusts, turbulence, a low-level shear, a microburst on final, the visibility and fog, up to three cloud layers, rain or snow, lightning, the runway's state, the temperature and the QNH. It applies at once in flight. The setup picks a preset for the flight, clear by default. The wind is felt as a crosswind, a headwind or a tailwind, and the altimeter is real: each flight starts with the day's QNH set, K sets it again, and the BARO box on the FCU or the MCP sets it too (scroll it, type into it, or STD). Above the transition altitude (14,000 ft) with a QNH set, or below FL140 on STD in a descent, the setting flashes until you change it. The landing card and the logbook give the wind at touchdown as the tower does. v0.4.2: a predictive windshear warning (WINDSHEAR AHEAD) for a microburst on the path ahead below 1,200 ft; hydroplaning above about 130 kt on standing water in heavy rain; wipers in the cockpit, spray off the wheels, and snow over the taxiway paint.

**Weather by place** (v0.7.4): the departure and the destination each have their own weather. New scenario's "Time and weather" picks both (the arrival's first choice is "Same as departure"), and the Weather tab's switch shows Both ends (the tab as it was), the departure or the destination, each changeable in flight, with the other end's METAR in a dim line under the one shown. Each end's weather holds over its third of the route, and across the middle third the two blend smoothly by position: the wind by height, the gusts and turbulence, the visibility, the cloud layers, the rain and snow, the temperature and the QNH, so the altimeter setting matters en route. A storm's microbursts and lightning stay near the end that has the storm. Every airport's METAR is its own: the two ends' from their weathers, any other's from the air at its place, each in its country's form. The haze, the clouds, the rain, a wet or snowy runway and the rain's sound follow the air where you are. The clouds keep one pattern and drift for the whole route, so crossing the blend changes their cover, bases, tops, rain and haze, never where they are. Change the route in flight and the destination's weather goes with it to the new destination. A circuit (the destination within 1 km of the departure) has one weather, the destination's, and the setup's Arrival picker is off for it. A save from before v0.7.4 has the same weather at both ends and flies as it did. **Since v0.7.9** the clouds are drawn where they are, so from the cruise you see the destination's weather ahead before you fly into it (`?wxahead79=0` draws the camera's own weather all round, as before), and the Weather tab's "Front" row (with two weathers) sets the boundary moving at 30 kt toward the departure or toward the destination: the weather at a place then changes with the clock.

From a gate:

1. Press P to push back. Q/E steer the turn, and P again holds the push.
2. Release the parking brake (B).
3. Follow the green-yellow line to the holding point.

After landing, a new line leads from the runway to a free stand. Every landing is scored on the touchdown itself (v0.6.2: the approach isn't scored, and only a hard touchdown holds the total down) and logged with its scenario: the Esc menu's Logbook shows this scenario's flights, the main menu's all of them with a filter. Each part (vertical speed, peak g, the touchdown point, the centreline, bank, pitch, bounces, the approach path, the sink rate's swings) scores out of its share of the 100 (v0.5.5: vertical speed, the touchdown point and the centreline the largest shares, bank and pitch the smallest), so the rows add up to the total; what comes off after that is a row of its own: the worst part holds the total to 50 + 10 × its score out of 10, the grass to 49, and a taxiway or a would-be crash halves it. 100 still takes a perfect landing in every part, but several middling flaws make a C or a D, not 0. Bounces are counted from the main wheels leaving the ground (v0.5.5). The centreline (v0.7.4) scores full marks within 1.5 m, then falls in a straight line to 0 where the outer main wheel's outer edge reaches the runway's edge, from the runway's own width and the flown aircraft's gear: about 18 m off for the A321 on a 45 m runway, 16 m for the 777, 25.5 m and 23.6 m on a 60 m runway (5.7 m in an A321 on 45 m scores 7 of 10). The logbook keeps the scores it recorded before.

Sound starts with the first click or key, because browsers don't play audio before then. Settings has the master volume, with four volumes under it since v0.7.4 (Warnings; Callouts volume, the voice and its ground-proximity and windshear calls; Engines; Airframe and ground: wind, gear, flaps, wheels, brakes, packs, rain, thunder and buffet), each 0 to 100 % of the master and saved, the size of the side panels (v0.6.2: 50 % to 120 %), the callouts and the performance readout, and (v0.4.1) two for practice: **Indestructible**, so no crash ends a flight (the landing card says what would have crashed; v0.4.2: the crash screen can turn it on as it rewinds), and **Boeing auto-trim**, so the 787 and 777 hold their flight path hands off and flare as the A321 does (v0.4.2); on final the nose stops within about a degree of where you let go, and pitches at 2.2°/s at most outside the flare and a go-around (v0.4.3).

## Flying tips

- **Take-off:**
  1. Hold R through the detents to TOGA (it pauses at CL and FLX/MCT).
  2. At about 145 kt, hold S. The aircraft rotates at about 4–5°/s and lifts off near 8°.
  3. G for gear up; − a step at a time to flaps 0 by about 210 kt.
  4. Pull the levers back to CL (F) for the autothrust.
- **One engine down** (or your own engine keys, v0.5.7): the aircraft yaws towards the low-thrust side, and a 787 or 777 rolls into it too. Hold it with the rudder (Q or E) and, on a Boeing, the wheel: about 4° of rudder at 250 kt, 13–16° on an approach (v0.5.8; the real Boeings' TAC, which does it for you, isn't modelled). The A/THR brings split levers back together, so try it with the A/THR off.
- **Autopilot:** set HDG and ALT on the FCU (scroll, or click and type; Enter on ALT starts the level change, OP CLB/OP DES), then Z (AP) and X (A/THR). The FCU heading starts at your heading. Time compression (=) needs the AP on and 2,000 ft above the ground.
- **Autothrust:** while A/THR is on it moves the thrust levers, and the blue ring on the N1 dials with them ("A/THR" between the dials). R or F takes the levers from where it left them and turns A/THR off; X turns it back on.
- **The map (M):** with the AP on, the bright line is where the aircraft will go. Click a point on it to jump there. The magenta line is the route to your destination and doesn't jump: point the HDG along it first. A red cross means the path meets the terrain. "Skip to 15 nm final" puts you on the 3° path to the destination runway.
  - **Waypoints:** drag the magenta line to add one; double-click a waypoint to remove it. Hover over the magenta line for the heading to set to follow it, worked out for the wind where you are (v0.6.2).
  - **Change route** (or a click on an airport) picks a new destination and runway in flight, with the distance, bearing and fuel needed direct from where you are. The line then goes direct from where you are.
- **Landing:**
  1. Gear down; flaps 2, 3, FULL; Shift+C to arm the ground spoilers.
  2. Disconnect the AP with Z.
  3. With flaps 3 or FULL, W/S nudge the flight path, and it holds where you leave it.
  4. From about 30 ft, hold S for a second or two to flare. The nose sinks by itself if you don't.
  5. Pull the levers to idle when you hear RETARD.
  6. On the ground, hold F for reverse and Space for the brakes.
- **Levers at TOGA** start a go-around at any point.
- **On a Boeing:** trim to the speed you want before you let go (H nose up, Y nose down), or turn on Boeing auto-trim in Settings and fly the path as on the A321. Pull the flare yourself from about 30 ft, and there's no RETARD call. With APP armed and the autopilot on it lands itself, and hands back below 30 kt: you brake (there's no autobrake).

## Failures (v0.8.0)

Update 8 starts to lift the game's first rule of "no aircraft systems": things can now fail, on command or by surprise. v0.8.0 has the engines, the gear, the hydraulics and the flight controls' degraded laws, and jammed flaps and slats; air data, the cabin, birds and tyres follow in later sub-updates.

- **The Failures tab** (the L window, `\` or Cmd+L, beside Sky, Weight and Weather): pick a failure by system (Engines, Gear, Hydraulics, Flaps and slats), its target (the engine, 1 to as many as the aircraft has; the leg: nose, left main, right main; the hydraulic system by its name) and when:
  - **Now**;
  - **at a moment:** on the take-off roll above 100 kt (the game shows no V1: this stands for it), at rotation, after lift-off, climbing through 10,000 ft, in cruise, on approach below 3,000 ft, on short final below 500 ft;
  - **after a time** (minutes and seconds);
  - **at random within a window:** tick the kinds it may be and give the minutes. The tab then says only "A random failure armed, within N minutes": not which, not when. The predicted path doesn't show it, and a jump past its moment stops just before it.

  The tab lists what is armed (with Disarm) and what has failed (with Repair, and Repair all). It works at the gate, in flight and while paused.
- **What is armed belongs to the flight, what has failed to the aircraft.** A rewind or a jump keeps what you have armed and takes the aircraft back, so a failure set for a time or a moment happens again when you fly through it again. A repair removes the failure for good. A saved scenario keeps both. A new flight starts with nothing armed; a skip to final keeps what has failed (fail an engine in cruise, skip to final, and land on one).
- **The warnings:** a red MASTER WARN and an amber MASTER CAUT light by the PFD (WARNING and CAUTION on the Boeings), with Airbus's repetitive chime for a warning and single chime for a caution, and the Boeings' fire bell, siren and caution beeper, all synthesised. Click a light (or SILENCE, or `'`) to cancel the sound and the light, as in the aircraft; the message stays. The message is on the engine display: the A321's as ECAM's (the title in red or amber, the actions in cyan), the Boeings' as an EICAS list.
- **The checklist:** each failure opens its procedure in short form beside the engine display. A step that is a switch is a button that does it (ENG MASTER OFF, the fire handle, AGENT 1, the gravity extension); a step that is flying (the thrust lever to idle, the gear lever) ticks itself when it is true. Fold the panel away with its tab. No overhead panel and no new keys.
- **The engines.** Each engine has its switch under its gauges on the engine display: ENG MASTER on the A321, FUEL CONTROL on the Boeings, one under each engine of a design. Click it to cut the engine; click again to start it.
  - **A start** takes about a minute on the ground. In the air it works inside the start envelope `[EST]`: below FL270 on the A321 (FL300 on the Boeings), windmilling at 260 kt or more (250 kt) in about 30 s, or with the starter's help below FL200 in about 45 s. Outside it the engine doesn't light and the checklist says why (too high, or too slow to windmill).
  - **A flameout:** the engine runs down and windmills, dragging. Switch it off and on to relight it.
  - **A seizure:** N1 falls to 0 in seconds, with more drag and no restart.
  - **A fire:** the red warning and its sound; the engine keeps running until you shut it down, and trails smoke (`?smoke80=0` on the page's address turns the smoke off). The checklist: the lever to idle, the switch off, the fire handle, agent 1, agent 2 if it still burns after 30 s, land at the nearest suitable airport. The first bottle puts most fires out, the second some, and a few burn on: that fire stays a warning to the landing (it damages nothing in this version).
  - With an engine out the autopilot and autothrust carry on as each type's laws already do; the yaw is yours to hold.
- **The gear.** Each leg now has its own position.
  - **The normal extension fails:** the doors open and the legs stay up. The checklist's gravity extension (the A321's hand crank, the Boeings' ALTN GEAR) drops them in about 30 s, and the gear then stays down. The gravity extension can be failed too.
  - **A leg that won't lock** stays up whatever you do; **a leg that collapses** does so at the next touchdown or a few seconds into the roll.
  - **The outside view and the gear lights show which leg is missing:** on the A321's LDG GEAR a red triangle for a leg that isn't locked down, on the Boeings' EICAS three boxes (left, nose, right) reading DN or UP.
  - **A landing with the gear up or a leg missing** (failed, or forgotten) slides to a stop on the belly and the nacelles, if you fly it on gently (sparks under the scraping parts are there behind `?sparks80=1` on the page's address, off by default: they don't look right yet): within about 10 ft/s of sink, 15° of bank and a normal landing attitude `[EST]`. The landing card says what kind of landing it was. Harder than that it is a crash, as before. Indestructible (Settings) keeps its own behaviour.
- **The hydraulics.** Each type has its three systems: green, blue and yellow on the A321 (engine 1's pump on green, engine 2's on yellow, an electric pump and the RAT on blue, the PTU between green and yellow, and a yellow electric pump the checklist can switch on); left, centre and right on the Boeings (an engine's pump and an electric demand pump on each side, electric pumps and the RAT in the centre, air-driven pumps too on the 777). A design's engines feed the side they hang on. The Failures tab can fail an engine's pump, a system's electric pumps, or drain a whole system; two systems drained is "two systems lost".
  - **What a lost system takes with it** `[EST]`: the gear's normal extension and retraction (use the gravity extension), the normal brakes (the alternate ones are weaker and have no anti-skid; with both gone the accumulator's), the nose-wheel steering, an engine's reverser, some of the spoilers, half the flaps' or slats' speed (all of it with both their systems gone), and a share of each control surface's speed.
  - **An engine failure costs its pump.** On the A321 the PTU then carries the other side's pressure across; on the Boeings the electric demand pump takes over.
  - **The RAT** drops out by itself in flight when every engine has stopped or two systems are lost, takes about 8 s to come up to speed, and powers blue on the A321 or the centre system's flight controls on the Boeings above about 130 kt. It stays out until repaired.
  - **The degraded laws.** With two systems lost the A321 goes to alternate law (no protections), and to direct law once the gear is down: the stick moves the surfaces directly, nothing trims by itself ("USE MAN PITCH TRIM" on the PFD: the trim keys), and the autopilot is gone. The Boeings go to secondary mode with two systems lost (no envelope protection, no autopilot) and to direct mode with all three.
  - **Flaps and slats jammed** stay where they are whatever the lever says. The approach speed on the PFD is the real one for where they are, the checklist gives it with the landing distance's factor, and the landing is longer and faster.
- **With no failure armed and the gear down nothing has changed:** the flight model is the same to the last bit.

**Flying a Boeing:** trim for the speed you want (H, Y), and it holds that speed hands off. Thrust moves the nose, and flaps and gear slow you only if you trim down too. Flare with back pressure; nothing does it for you. With Boeing auto-trim on (Settings), it's the A321's way instead: let go and the flight path holds, the thrust (or the autothrottle) sets the speed, and with the flaps for landing W and S nudge the path.

## The warehouse (v0.5.0, parts anywhere since v0.5.2)

**Warehouse** on the main menu, after Load scenario: your own aircraft, each built on one of the three types (its base: its wing, fuselage, tail and limits). Mouse and touchpad, and a few keys of its own.

- **The list:** New from the A321neo, the 787-9 or the 777-300ER; Open (or click the row), Rename, Duplicate, Delete.
- **The editor** (v0.5.2, KSP's way): the aircraft in 3D in the middle (drag to go round it, pinch or scroll to zoom, double-click to recentre), the **parts palette** over its left edge, and the side panel on the right.
  - **Attach:** pick a part in the palette (the LEAP-1A, the GEnx-1B or the GE90-115B; a wing tank, a hold tank, ballast) and move the pointer over the aircraft: its ghost follows, snapped to where it would go (0.25 m steps), green where it fits and red where it doesn't (another part in the way, the limits, a second engine in the tail), with its name and place beside the pointer. Click to attach it; Shift+click keeps it in hand for another; right-click or Esc puts it back. Or drag it from the palette onto the aircraft and let go (v0.5.6); let go off the aircraft, or where it doesn't fit, and it goes back. The selected part's keys work on the part in hand too (v0.5.6): ↑ ↓ fore and aft, ← → out and in from where the pointer puts it, Shift+↑ ↓ an engine up and down, Q E turn it a degree, with the values beside the pointer; Shift+click keeps them for the next one.
  - **Where engines go:** dropped on the wing, under it or over it (the palette's Under / Over); on the rear fuselage's sides; on the fin, one in the tail. Up to 8 engines and 24 parts.
  - **Mirror** (on by default) attaches a twin on the other side and keeps the pair together whichever you edit; turn it off for one side alone. A part's own Mirror box pairs or unpairs it later.
  - **Select, move, remove:** click a part to select it (its box lights up, its twin's dimmer), drag it to move it (the ghost shows where), Delete or Backspace removes it (and its twin), Esc lets go. **Z** undoes and **Shift+Z** redoes every change (the buttons at the top right too); a slider's drag is one step. The name and registration aren't steps. **Keys** (v0.5.4) for the selected part: ↑ ↓ forward and aft, ← → out and in along the span, Shift+↑ ↓ an engine up and down, Q E turn an engine a degree; each press is one step, a held key's repeats one between them. A renamed design is renamed in the scenarios that fly it.
  - **A part's settings** (the side panel, while it's selected): its mount, its engine, where it sits (out from the centreline, or back from the nose), nudged forward or aft (±3 m) and up or down (±1 m), toed in or out and pitched (±10°), its thrust (0.5 to 3 times its type's), or a tank's fuel (1,000 to 8,000 kg under the wing, to 3,000 kg in the hold) and the ballast's mass (100 to 6,000 kg).

While a slider moves, the new outline is drawn in green over the model, which follows when you let go. A slider's drag is one undo step; **As the base** puts that part back; Esc or right-click lets go. Engines and wing tanks ride the wing (and move in on a shorter one), and so does the main gear (v0.5.6: each leg where the stock leg is relative to the wing, so a smaller chord or less sweep brings it forward; the nose-wheel check says when to move the wing aft); the windows and doors are repeated in the plugs, not stretched.

The camera stays inside: zoomed out, it stops at the wall, the roof or the floor.

- **The airframe's numbers** (v0.5.3), each against the base's: the **static margin** at the default load (from the parts' neutral point), **Cnβ** (directional stability), the **stall** at MLW in landing flaps and the approach speed, the **tail-strike angle**, the **nose wheel's load** standing, and the **wingspan's code letter** against the stand's; v0.5.6: the **minimum control speed** (VMC: full rudder, the worst engine out and the rest at full thrust) against the take-off rotation speed (amber within 5 %, red above it), and for lopsided thrust the speed below which the rudder can't hold it with every engine running.
- **The checks:** amber warns, red will fail or touch:
  - **red (v0.5.3, the airframe):** the CG behind the neutral point (negative static margin), Cnβ at zero or below, under 2.5 % of the weight on the nose wheel (full thrust tips it onto its tail), the static margin 25 % MAC over the base's (too nose-heavy to rotate);
  - **amber (v0.5.3):** under 5 % MAC of static margin or 10 % over the base's, Cnβ under half the base's, a stall over 1.1 times the base's, the tail striking 1.5° sooner, under 5 % on the nose wheel, a span past the stand's code letter (such a design, or a stretched one, is offered only the stands that fit its own span and length, parks with its own nose on the stop mark, and the parked traffic makes room for its own wings). On the base's own airframe the CG against the base's (below) still shows; on a changed one the static margin takes its place;
  - **red:** no engines (Save and Fly it wait for one); a nacelle or tank touching the ground, standing or at the tail-strike attitude; parts overlapping; a pylon past its limit; thrust lopsided past an engine failure on the base (the rudder can't hold it on the runway); the CG more than 12 % MAC aft of the base's (past its static margin) or 25 % forward;
  - **amber:** under 0.3 m of clearance; a pylon past 90 %; lopsided thrust past a tenth of an engine failure; the CG 5 % aft or 10 % forward; the thrust's pitch 2.5 times the base's; thrust to weight over 0.8 when light (full power on the runway can lift the nose into a tail strike); little thrust (under 0.22 at MTOW); under half the base's payload room.
- **Fly it:** saved designs are cards in New scenario's aircraft picker, under the three types; the editor's **Fly it** saves the design and opens New scenario with it picked. A scenario keeps its own copy of the design, so editing or deleting it later doesn't change a saved flight. v0.5.0's designs open as they were.

**What it does in flight:**

- **Each part's weight** where it is: the CG and the inertias follow (ballast aft moves the CG aft; a long wing's engines roll it slower).
- **Each part's drag** where it is, against the airflow (a nacelle about CD 0.05 on its frontal area, a wing tank 0.08): a lone wing tank or engine far out yaws the nose towards its side.
- **Each engine's thrust along its own line:** toe-in on a pair cancels, a lone toed engine yaws, pitched engines push up or down with their moments. Mixed engines each run on their own numbers (thrust, spool, fuel flow) and sound like themselves.
- **Fuel in the tanks** joins the centre tank's (burned first), its CG the mean of where it is.
- **Off-centre thrust yaws:** an engine at idle, reversing alone or gone pulls the nose towards the dead side (on the runway, below about 110 kt, nothing holds it straight: reject the take-off).
- **The pylons:** each holds 1.5 times its engine type's rated take-off thrust (CS-25's ultimate factor), and each a little more as built (0 to 10 %). Past it the engine **tears off**: it stops, its weight goes, the model loses it and its pylon, and the screens show it OFF. Indestructible doesn't stop it (it's a failure, not a crash).
- **A big engine on a small aircraft scrapes:** two GE90s on an A321 sit a metre into the runway unless the gear is lengthened (1.5 m clears them by 0.4 m). They also give it a thrust-to-weight over 1 and a thrust line far below its centre of gravity, so full thrust on the runway pitches it into a tail strike: take off with about a third of the lever.
- **More engines burn more fuel** by the weight and drag they add: the fuel flow is the thrust's.
- **The screens and the sound:** the A321's engine panel and the Boeings' EICAS draw every engine (up to 8); the synth gives each its own voice.
- **The airframe from parts** (v0.5.3): the wing (each side), the tailplane, the fin and the fuselage are parts too, and the handling follows from them. Each wing, tailplane and fin panel is a few strips, each with its own airflow (the aircraft's motion and rotation at that strip, the wing's downwash at the tail, the sidewash at the fin), its own lift curve and stall, drag and moment; the fuselage adds its drag, side force and destabilising moments. A design flies its base's model plus what its parts change, so a bigger tailplane gives more static margin and a quicker short period, a smaller fin less directional stability and a Dutch roll that dies out more slowly, a longer span a steeper lift curve, less induced drag, more roll damping and a lower stall, one wing panel longer than the other a roll towards the short one. The structure weighs what its parts weigh where they are (a longer wing rolls slower, plugs add pitch and yaw inertia), the wing's tanks hold what its volume does, the engines and wing tanks ride the wing, a stretched body lowers the tail-strike angle, and the laws (the A321's normal law, the Boeings', the autopilots, the yaw dampers) tune themselves to the design's control power, so it flies with its base's feel. The three types themselves fly exactly as before.

## Storage

All in localStorage, under `gamedump:flight-sim:`:

- `scenarios`: the scenario index (id, name, created, savedAt, a summary); `scenario:<id>`: each scenario's whole state (a few KB, up to about 70 KB with a long approach track);
- `logbook`: every flight, tagged with its scenario's id and name (v0.1.x's untagged flights count as Scenario 1's);
- `settings` (volume, callouts, the readout, the setup's last plan; v0.7.4: the four volumes `volWarnings`, `volCallouts`, `volEngines` and `volFrame`, a missing one read as 100 %) and `lastflight` (the last flight's plan);
- `designs` (v0.5.0): the warehouse's designs, one list (v0.5.2: each a list of parts; v0.5.0's open as they were);
- `region` (v0.7.0): the last region used. Each scenario carries its region in its entry and its state (East Asia's are written as before, with no field). Switching region carries the scenario to start in sessionStorage, under `gamedump:flight-sim:region-carry`.
