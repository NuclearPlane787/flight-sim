## The rules every part keeps

- **Calm is exact.** With the default weather (calm, ISA, QNH 1013.25 hPa, the baro at 1013.25), the sim does the same arithmetic as v0.3.2, not just nearly the same numbers:
  - the golden A321 flight (`tests/golden-a321.test.mjs`) matches to 1e-9;
  - all 521 unit tests pass with their assertions unchanged (imports may move).

  Skip each new term when it's zero, or make it exactly zero. Adding a wind of exactly 0 is exact, but a rewritten ISA formula or a pressure-to-altitude round trip isn't.
- **Seeded and stateless, so the predicted path and the jumps stay exact.**
  - The live sim steps at 1/120 s (`STEP` in `main.js`), the prediction worker at 1/30 s (`PREDICT_DEFAULTS`), and a jump is `sim.restore()` of a worker snapshot.
  - So a filter stepped once per step would give the two different gusts. Make gusts and turbulence functions of position and time instead: a frozen field (Taylor's hypothesis), seeded, carried with the mean wind. Either a spectral sum shaped to the Dryden spectrum or smooth seeded lattice noise will do. Lightning's times and places are seeded the same way.
  - Then the snapshot only needs the weather's settings and its seed.
  - No `Math.random()` anywhere the sim or the prediction reads.
- **Snapshots carry the weather.**
  - `sim.snapshot()` holds the weather's settings, so scenario saves, Duplicate, Save a copy and the prediction all carry it.
  - `restore()` of a save without weather gives calm (every v0.3.2 save flies on exactly as before).
  - What a rewind does with weather set since that moment is yours to decide. Suggested: the weather set now stays, since it's the world's, not the aircraft's. Log the choice.
- **One weather everywhere** (horizontally uniform), apart from the features that sit at a place: microbursts and storm cells. Moving weather and weather by region are later versions.
- **Directions as the tower gives them:** in degrees magnetic, "from", in knots, on the panel, the NDs and the card. Inside, convert with `src/world/frame.js` (`magToGrid()` and the rest).
- **The CPU rules at runtime** (`PLAN.md` → CPU at runtime):
  - 60 fps at 1440×900 on the M5;
  - nothing runs while paused or hidden;
  - the prediction stays within about 2 s of CPU with turbulence on: time it.
- **Keys:** K is new (the local QNH). No Ctrl bindings (Cmd/Ctrl+L stays the only one). Update the Controls page and `README.md`.

## Part 1: the weather in the sim, without visuals

1. **`src/sim/weather.js`** (no DOM; the worker imports it): the settings, as a plain JSON-safe object, and the functions the sim calls.
2. **The mean wind by height:**
   - from the panel's surface wind (at 10 m) and its wind aloft;
   - a boundary layer near the ground (a log or power law, with the veer over land);
   - blending up to the wind aloft;
   - an optional low-level shear (a sharp change across a thin layer).
3. **Gusts:** seeded, to the panel's gust speed, with realistic timing. They're what the windsock and the card call "G".
4. **Turbulence:** none, light, moderate or severe. Dryden, as in MIL-F-8785C (or MIL-HDBK-1797):
   - Below 1,000 ft (h in ft): L_w = h, L_u = L_v = h / (0.177 + 0.000823 h)^1.2, σ_w = 0.1 W20, σ_u = σ_v = σ_w / (0.177 + 0.000823 h)^0.4. W20, the wind at 20 ft, is 15 kt for light, 30 kt for moderate and 45 kt for severe.
   - Above 2,000 ft: L = 1,750 ft, with σ from the standard's exceedance curves (look them up; don't guess).
   - Between the two, interpolate.

   The gust velocities act at the CG. Angular gust terms (a roll gust especially, for the feel) are yours to decide.
5. **Wind shear and microbursts as fields in space.**
   - A microburst is an axisymmetric downburst. Oseguera and Bowles' analytic model (NASA TM-100632, 1988) or Vicroy's (NASA TM-104053) are the usual simple ones.
   - Typical numbers to check against the sources: about 1 to 4 km across, with a headwind-to-tailwind change of 40 kt or more.
   - The panel can put one on the arrival runway's final, at a chosen distance and strength. The thunderstorm preset scatters seeded cells near the airports, each a microburst under its rain shaft.
6. **Temperature and pressure** (`src/sim/atmosphere.js`):
   - an ISA deviation (from the panel's sea-level temperature) and the QNH;
   - pressure hydrostatic through the ISA + ΔT column, with the QNH at sea level. At Korea's low airports, an altimeter set to the QNH then reads within a few metres of the field's elevation. Colder air makes it read high aloft: the cold-temperature error, as in reality.
   - density changes lift and thrust, the speed of sound changes the Mach number, and TAS changes for a given IAS.
   - Engines lapse with σ and Mach (`engines.js`). A flat rating (constant take-off thrust up to about ISA + 15) is a **Should**.
7. **The air-relative velocity in `step()`:**
   - `u`, `v`, `w` become the velocity relative to the air (inertial less wind, in the body frame). `V`, `alpha`, `beta`, `ias`, `mach` and `qbar` come from that.
   - `kctx.u`/`v`/`w` for `legStep()` stay inertial: the gear rolls on the ground, not in the air.
   - `updateState()`'s `tas` is the airspeed; `gs` stays inertial. Add the wind (vector and parts) and the drift to `state`.
   - Everything that computes the IAS from the inertial speed uses the airspeed: `reset()`, `restore()` and `setGroundPose()` in `sim.js`.
   - `trimAirborne()` (a final or an airborne start) trims in the air mass: the inertial velocity is the air velocity plus the wind, crabbed so that the track is the one asked for (on a final, the runway's).
8. **The indicated altitude:** from the static pressure and the baro setting (STD is 1013.25).
   - The PFDs' altitude, the FCU/MCP altitude targets, ALT HOLD, V/S, FLCH and the altitude captures and alerts all use it.
   - The radio altimeter, the height callouts, the score, the map and the terrain stay geometric.
   - In calm with the baro at 1013.25, the indicated altitude must be exactly the old altitude (see "Calm is exact").
9. **Tests** (`tests/weather*.test.mjs`): the unit tests in the definition of done below, and the model's own: the profile, the turbulence's σ and scale lengths measured off the field, the microburst's outflow, the ISA + ΔT column, QNH and the indicated altitude.

## Part 2: the autopilots and the screens

1. **The Boeings fly the track.** LNAV (about lines 296 to 313 of `autopilot-boeing.js`, `psiCmd = atan2(dx, dy)`) and LOC (about 351, `ils.hdgGrid − atan(xte / LOC_L)`) command a heading today, and the bank follows `2.2 × (psiCmd − psi)` (about 354). In a crosswind the localizer would sit about `LOC_L × tan(drift)` off: some 100 m at 5° of drift.
   - Correct the command by the drift (track less heading), or command the track.
   - G/S already uses the groundspeed.
   - HDG SEL and HDG HOLD stay headings (they drift with the wind, as they should). So do the A321's holds (`autopilot.js`).
2. **The autoland decrabs.** Find out how Boeing's autoland lines up in a crosswind (a runway alignment that begins with a forward slip and takes the rest of the crab out in the flare, as far as Claude knows: check it), and do that. ROLLOUT holds the centreline with the rudder and the nosewheel, with aileron into the wind. It always tries (decision 7).
3. **The NDs show the wind** (`src/ui/nd.js` about 193, `src/ui/boeing/nd.js` about 192: "no wind is modelled, so no wind readout"):
   - each maker's way: direction and speed, and the arrow;
   - the track on the ND and on the PFD's heading scale (the A321's diamond, the Boeings' track line), so the drift shows.
4. **The PFDs and the baro** (`src/ui/pfd.js` 330 to 332 print "QNH 1013"; `src/ui/boeing/pfd.js` 355 to 356 print "1013 HPA"):
   - the setting in each maker's style, and STD;
   - the BARO box on the FCU (`src/ui/fcu.js`) and the MCP (`src/ui/boeing/mcp.js`), in their existing typing and scrolling style;
   - **K** sets the local QNH (the weather's, since it's the same everywhere).
   - **Should:** the transition reminder in each maker's style when QNH is still set climbing through Korea's transition altitude (14,000 ft as far as Claude knows: check the eAIP, ENR 1.7), or STD still set descending through the transition level.
5. **Windshear warnings,** reactive, on all three types:
   - from the F-factor, F = (dWx/dt)/g − Wh/V, averaged over about a kilometre of flight;
   - the threshold from FAA TSO-C117a: look it up;
   - each maker's annunciation, through `src/audio/callouts.js` and the PFDs (Airbus: "WINDSHEAR" three times, a red WINDSHEAR on the PFD; Boeing: the siren, "WINDSHEAR", a red WINDSHEAR on the PFD): check both.

   A predictive warning ("WINDSHEAR AHEAD", from the radar's view of a cell) is a **Nice**.
6. **The wind at touchdown** (decision 6):
   - on the landing card (`src/ui/score-panel.js`) and in the logbook (`src/ui/logbook.js`, a column): the wind, its crosswind part (with its side) and its head or tailwind part, relative to the runway landed on;
   - kept by the scorer (`src/score.js`) with the touchdown;
   - the score's costs don't change: a crosswind is harder, not scored differently.
7. **The test pilots:**
   - `tests/e2e-controller.js` crabs on the approach (it tracks the localizer by its track, not its heading) and decrabs in the flare. It already decrabs with the rudder at about line 109.
   - `tests/browser-keyboard-landing.mjs` (real key events only) learns the same with Q and E and the wing-down A and D, as a person on the keyboard would.

## Part 3: the runways

- **Dry, wet, snow-covered and icy,** as friction factors on the side grip, the braking and the rolling resistance (`src/sim/gear.js` about 51 to 60). The grass factors (`SIDE_MU_GRASS`, `BRAKE_MU_GRASS`, `ROLL_RESIST_GRASS`) are the pattern.
- **Take the braking numbers from a source:** EASA AMC 25.1591 and the FAA's runway condition codes (TALPA) give the aircraft braking coefficient by runway condition. Snow and slush also add drag on the wheels.
- **The runway's state:** automatic from the precipitation and the temperature (rain makes it wet, snow below freezing covers it, freezing rain ices it), or set on the panel. It's the same at every airport.
- **Hydroplaning** (above about 9 √(tyre pressure, psi) kt on standing water) is a **Nice**.

## Part 4: the look, by day and at night

1. **Visibility and fog:**
   - `HAZE_SIGMA0` and `HAZE_HEIGHT` (`src/world/sky.js`) are constants written into the shaders today. Make them uniforms.
   - The visibility sets the extinction by Koschmieder's law, σ = 3.912 / visibility (2.6e-5 per metre is about 150 km).
   - A fog layer has a top. Inside a cloud the visibility is tens of metres.
   - **Every shader that hazes takes them:** `terrain.js`, `sky.js` (the sky, the sun and the stars greying out too), `airports.js`, `apron.js`, `cities.js`, `citylights.js` (which repeats the integral), `detail.js` and `traffic.js`; and `scene.fog` (`sky.js` about 548 to 551) for three's own materials, the aircraft among them.
   - `airports.js` (about 546) hides runways beyond 80 km, the haze's reach. In fog it must still show the approach lights a pilot would see at that visibility.
2. **Cloud layers:** flat, up to three, each with its cover (FEW, SCT, BKN, OVC, in oktas), base and top.
   - Lit by the sun and the Moon; at night, dark above and lit orange from below over the cities.
   - Through a layer, the view goes white or grey, then clears above it. Above an overcast it's a white floor in the sun.
3. **The overcast light** (`lightingAt()`):
   - under cloud, the sun's direct light falls with the cover and the ambient turns grey and dimmer;
   - above it, full sun.
4. **Rain:**
   - streaks around the camera, slanted by the airspeed;
   - drops on the windshield in the cockpit views;
   - the wet runway darker, with a sheen.
   - **Should:** the lights reflecting off a wet runway at night. **Nice:** spray from the wheels, and wipers (a key if they're added, logged).
5. **Snow:**
   - flakes, which streak at speed;
   - snow on the ground: the land cover whitened, the runways white or cleared by their state, the cities brighter under it at night.
6. **Lightning:**
   - flashes inside the clouds, lighting the layer and the scene for a moment;
   - bolts to the ground for strikes nearby;
   - seeded (see the rules).
7. **Budgets:** each particle system is one draw (instanced or points), sized for 60 fps in heavy rain at night. `PLAN.md` → CPU at runtime and the night pitfalls in `HANDOFF.md` apply (night needs every ground shader; night-only objects hide by day). Weather-only objects hide in clear weather, so clear weather's draw calls equal v0.3.2's.
