## Shane's decisions (2026-11-14)

The Cowork session asked; he picked these.

1. **The physics: handling from parts.** Each wing, tail and fuselage part makes its own lift and drag at its own place, and the aircraft's handling (its stability, its damping, its control power, its stall) follows from the parts and where they are. **It stays one rigid body:** no flex and no breaking in this version (Besiege's side, the structure bending and failing, comes later).
2. **The split:** v0.5.2 in the Cowork session (done: the KSP-style editor, parts attached anywhere on the three stock airframes, each part's mass, drag and thrust line), v0.5.3 in this run (the airframe itself from parts).
3. **When:** Shane starts this run as soon as v0.5.2 is pushed (10:30 KST, Saturday 14 November 2026).

**Decided in the Cowork session** (where Shane's words leave a choice; change nothing here without a reason, and log it if you do):

- **The three types fly exactly as they do now.** Their lumped coefficient model (`src/sim/flightmodel.js`) stays, untouched, and so do their golden flights. The parts' model is for designs.
- **A design's aerodynamics: the base's model, plus the parts' difference.** For a design, every coefficient is `lumped(base, state) + parts(design, state) − parts(base, state)`, where `parts()` sums each part's own forces and moments at the current state. So a design that is its base flies its golden flight to 1e-9 (as since v0.5.0), and a changed part changes the handling by what that part changes. The parts' model must still be good on its own: `parts(base)` within a few per cent of `lumped(base)` for each type (Part 1's validation table) is the evidence that the handling comes from the parts and not from the residual.
- **The airframe's parts are the base's parts, changed:** the wing (each side), the tailplane, the fin and the fuselage, each with a few sizes to change (Part 2). Swapping in another type's wing, canards and winglets are extras, only if everything else is done.
- **The flight controls tune themselves** to the design (Part 3), so a design with a bigger tail or a longer wing still flies with the same feel on the A321's laws and the Boeings'.

## Part 1: the parts' aerodynamics (first, and alone)

A new pure module, `src/sim/parts-aero.js` (no DOM, no three.js; the sim and the prediction worker import it):

1. **The parts from a type's geometry:** the wing as two panels (left, right), the tailplane as two, the fin, the fuselage, each from the type's data and geometry (`src/geometry.js`, `widebody-specs.js`, the `sim/` data modules): spans, chords, sweep, dihedral, incidence, positions, areas. The tails' areas and arms where the data modules lack them: published figures with the source, or `[EST]` with the reasoning, as the data modules do.
2. **Each lifting panel as a few spanwise strips** (say 4 to 6 per wing panel, 2 or 3 per tail panel and for the fin), each with its own local airflow: the body's velocity plus ω × r at the strip (so roll damping, pitch damping, yaw damping and the dihedral effect come out of the strips, not from tabled derivatives), the wing's downwash at the tailplane (lagged for the α-dot term) and the sidewash at the fin. Each strip: its section's lift slope (corrected for the panel's aspect ratio and sweep), its stall (per strip, so a wing stalls from where it should), its profile and induced drag, its moment about its quarter chord. The control surfaces deflect the strips they cover: the ailerons, the elevator and the stabiliser, the rudder, the flaps and slats, the spoilers.
3. **The fuselage:** its drag, its side force and its destabilising moments in pitch and yaw (Munk's), from its length and width.
4. **The nacelles' and tanks' drag** are v0.5.2's `DRAG_PARTS`: keep them, inside `parts()` or beside it.
5. **The validation table:** for each type, `parts(base)` against `lumped(base)` at cruise and at approach (clean, and flaps 3 or 30): CLα, CL at the trimmed α, CD at that CL, Cmα (and the static margin), Cmq, CY β, Cnβ, Clβ, Clp, Cnr, and the elevator's, the ailerons' and the rudder's power. Within a few per cent each, or explained in `STATUS.md` (the lumped model's own numbers have sources: where the parts disagree with a sourced number, the parts are what's wrong).
6. **The design's coefficients,** `lumped(base) + parts(design) − parts(base)`, wired into `sim.js` for designs only. The stock types don't call it.

**Done when** the table is in `STATUS.md` with every row within a few per cent (or explained), a design that is its base flies its golden flight to 1e-9, the 744 old tests pass unchanged, and a design with a tailplane 30 % bigger (set by hand in a test, before Part 2's warehouse exists) has the static margin and the short period you'd expect. Checkpoint in `STATUS.md`. Parts 2 to 5 can then go to subagents in parallel, once the `airframe` field's shape (Part 2's first step) is fixed.

## Part 2: the airframe as data

1. **The `airframe` field** (example; yours to shape): the wing's span (0.8 to 1.3 of the base's), chord (0.8 to 1.3), sweep (−8° to +8° from the base's) and dihedral (−3° to +8°); the tailplane's and the fin's areas (0.6 to 1.6 of the base's); the fuselage's plugs ahead of and behind the wing (−4 to +6 m each). A wing panel each side may differ (KSP would let you): if you allow it, the warehouse's Mirror keeps them together by default.
2. **What follows from it,** in `designDef()`:
   - the geometry: `WING`, `SPAN`, the tails, `LENGTH`, `NOSE_X`, `TAIL_STRIKE_POINT`, the gear's place against the wing, the crash points and Indestructible's skids;
   - the mass: each part's structure weight scaled from the base's (a standard estimate: the wing's with its area and span, the tails' with their areas, the plugs' per metre; `[EST]` fractions of OEW with the reasoning), and the inertias (a longer wing rolls slower);
   - the fuel: the wing's tanks with the wing's volume;
   - the mounts: v0.5.2's `airframe(base)` (`src/sim/designs.js`) becomes the design's airframe, so engines and tanks stay where they're attached as the wing changes (a station past a shorter wing's limit is clamped, as normalising does);
   - the data the lumped model reads that a part changes (`S`, `SPAN`, `MAC`, `AR`, the tail strike angle, the wing height for ground effect).
3. **The checks** (`designNumbers()` and the warehouse's `readout()`): the static margin from the parts' neutral point against the CG at the default load (red below zero, amber below 5 % MAC: this replaces v0.5.2's "CG against the base's" row), Cnβ (red at zero or below), the stall speed at MLW against the base's (the approach speed follows), the tail-strike angle with the plugs, the wingspan against the gate's code letter.

## Part 3: the controls tune themselves

The A321's normal law (`src/sim/fbw.js`), the Boeings' (`fbw-boeing.js`, auto-trim, the flare law) and both autopilots were tuned for their types. For a design, scale their gains by the design's control power and inertia against the base's (for example the elevator's Cm per radian and Iyy for pitch; the ailerons' Cl and Ixx for roll; the rudder's Cn and Izz for the yaw damper and the turn coordination), computed once in `designDef()` from Part 1's numbers. A design that is its base gets exactly 1, so its golden flight holds.

**Tests:** a design with the tailplane at 0.7 and at 1.5 of the base's, and one with the wing's span at 1.25: each trims, holds a 30° bank turn, captures an altitude and flares within the same limits as its base (`tests/e2e-controller.js`, or the performance tests' scripted pilot in node).

## Part 4: the model follows

1. **The wing:** `aircraft.js` (the A321's, from `geometry.js`'s `WING`) and `widebody.js` (from `widebody-specs.js`) build their wings from the design's geometry: span, chord, sweep and dihedral, the flaps, slats, ailerons and spoilers with it, the winglets at the new tips, the Boeings' wing bend over the new span.
2. **The tails:** the tailplane and the fin scaled to their areas (aspect ratio and sweep kept), the elevator and rudder with them.
3. **The fuselage:** the plugs lengthen it ahead of and behind the wing (the windows, doors and livery stretched or repeated, not distorted), the tail cone and tail moving back with the rear plug.
4. **The gear** stays under the wing's root, which may move with the forward plug.
5. Look at it: each type at the limits of each slider, in the warehouse and in flight with the wing bent.
